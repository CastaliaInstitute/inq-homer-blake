## Translation

At dawn the swineherd and the long-enduring king
Prepared their breakfast and sent the herdsmen out.
The dogs, who barked at every stranger, fawned
Upon Telemachus and made no sound. Odysseus heard
The tread of feet: “Eumaeus, one you know comes near.”
Before he finished, Telemachus stood within the door.
The swineherd dropped the vessels, ran, and kissed
His head, his eyes, and hands, shedding a warm tear,
As a father greets an only son returned
After ten years of hardship in a distant land.

“You have come, sweet light,” he cried. “I did not think
To see you again, since you sailed away to Pylos.”
Telemachus asked whether his mother remained,
Or whether another Achaean possessed the bed
Of absent Odysseus. “She remains,” said Eumaeus,
“Enduring in her heart; her nights and days
Waste out in tears.” He gave his spear to Telemachus,
Who crossed the stone threshold. Odysseus yielded his seat;
Telemachus held him back as a stranger. Eumaeus spread
Green brushwood, fleece, roasted meat, bread, and wine,
And the three ate together.

Telemachus asked how the stranger reached Ithaca.
Eumaeus said he was a Cretan, escaped from a Thesprotian ship,
And placed him in the young king's hands. Telemachus answered:
“How shall I receive him? I am young and cannot yet trust
My hands against a man who strikes first. My mother debates
Whether to remain beside me and guard the house,
Or follow the best Achaean, the one who brings most gifts.
But since he came to your door, I will clothe and send him on.
Do not let him enter the suitors' crowd; their insolence
Might mock him, and it would bring me unbearable grief.”

Odysseus asked why Telemachus endured the suitors:
“Are you hated by the people, or do you reproach your kin?
Would that I were young again—or your father's son—
Then I would enter the house and become a plague to all.
Better to die in my own halls than watch guests beaten,
Women dragged through rooms, wine spilled, and food consumed.”

Telemachus answered that no people hated him and he had no brothers:
The family had been made a single thread by Zeus—Arcesius to Laertes,
Laertes to Odysseus, Odysseus to him. Yet enemies without number
Consumed the house: men from Ithaca, Dulichium, Same, and Zacynthus.
Penelope neither accepted nor could complete the hated marriage.
“Eumaeus, go tell my mother that I am safe and home from Pylos.
I will remain here; let no other Achaean know, for many plot my harm.”

Eumaeus asked whether he should also tell old Laertes.
Telemachus said it was painful, but a hidden message might reach him.
The swineherd went toward the city in sandals. Athena came near the hut
In the form of a tall and beautiful woman. Only Odysseus and the dogs saw her.
She nodded, and said: “Tell your son now. Conceal nothing.
Plan death and doom for the suitors, then go together to the city.”

With her golden wand she restored Odysseus's clean cloak and tunic,
Increased his body and youth, darkened his skin, and spread
A dark beard around his chin. She departed. Odysseus entered.
Telemachus recoiled: “You are changed; perhaps you are a god.
Be gracious, and we will give you sacrifices and gold.”
“I am no god,” said Odysseus. “I am your father,
For whose sake you suffer.” He kissed him, and a tear fell.
Telemachus doubted: “Some god deceives me. No mortal could devise
This change.” “No other Odysseus will come,” the father answered.
“I am he, home after twenty years of suffering and wandering.
Athena can make me young or old as she wishes.”

Telemachus threw his arms around him; both wept together,
With cries sharper than birds of prey when farmers steal their young.
He asked how Odysseus crossed the sea. “The Phaeacians carried me
Sleeping to Ithaca,” said the king, “and gave me bronze, gold, and cloth,
Stored in a cave by Athena's counsel.” He asked the number of the suitors.
Telemachus counted fifty-two from Ithaca, six servants from Dulichium,
Twenty-four from Same, twenty from Zacynthus, twelve more Ithacans,
The herald Medon, the singer, and two servants.

“Athena and Zeus will suffice,” said Odysseus. “At dawn go home
And mingle with them. Let Eumaeus bring me later as a beggar.
If they strike or drag me, endure it and do not defend me openly.
When Athena gives the sign, carry all the weapons to the upper chamber.
Tell the suitors smoke damaged them, and that wine might make them
Wound one another at the feast. Leave only two swords, two spears,
And two shields for us. Let no one—Laertes, Eumaeus, servant, or mother—
Know that I am here until we have tested the household.”

Telemachus agreed, but urged that the women be tested first.
While the father and son spoke, the ship reached the deep harbor.
Its servants carried the gifts to Clytius's house, and a herald went
To Penelope: Telemachus was safe in the country, and had ordered
The ship to sail to the city so she would not weep from fear.
The herald and Eumaeus reached her together; each gave the message.

The suitors lamented. Eurymachus urged them to fetch a ship,
Gather rowers, and summon their companions. Amphinomus saw the ship
Already entering and told them no messenger was needed. Antinous spoke:
“The gods freed Telemachus from our ambush. Kill him before he gathers
The people, or divide his wealth and give the house to his mother.”
Amphinomus refused to kill a king's son without asking the gods;
His counsel pleased them, and they returned to the house.

Penelope had heard their plot from Medon. She stood before them,
Veiled, and reproached Antinous for weaving death against her son,
Reminding him that Odysseus had once restrained his angry father.
Eurymachus assured her no man would harm Telemachus while he lived,
Though in his heart he prepared the son's destruction. Penelope went upstairs
And wept for Odysseus until Athena gave her sleep.

At evening Eumaeus came to the hut. Athena struck Odysseus again,
Making him old and ragged, lest Eumaeus recognize and tell the queen.
Telemachus asked what news he had brought. Eumaeus had hurried back
After the swift herald told Penelope. He had seen another thing:
A ship entering the harbor, heavy with shields and spears.
Telemachus smiled toward his father, avoiding Eumaeus's eyes.
They finished supper, remembered the bed, and received the gift of sleep.

## Decision log

- The recognition follows the source sequence: Eumaeus receives Telemachus;
  Athena appears to Odysseus and the dogs; Odysseus is restored, doubted,
  recognized, and only then begins the weapons plan.
- The suitor count and weapon instructions remain explicit and conditional on
  Athena's sign. The false smoke explanation and the danger of drunken quarrel
  are both retained.
- Theoclymenus does not receive a new omen in Book 16; his hawk belongs to
  Book 15. Here the herald and Eumaeus reach Penelope, the suitors debate,
  and Athena restores the beggar's form before Eumaeus returns.

  meter review, notes review, and production sign-off before promotion beyond
  `draft`.
