## Revised translation pass

But Poseidon had gone among the Ethiopians,
Far off, divided between the setting Sun
And where the Sun ascends—the furthest race of men—
To share their hecatomb of bulls and rams.
There he sat rejoicing at the feast, while all
The other gods were gathered in the halls of Zeus.

Then the father of gods and men began to speak;
For in his heart he thought upon blameless Aegisthus,
Whom far-renowned Orestes, Agamemnon’s son,
Had slain. Remembering him, he spoke among the immortals:

“Ah me! How mortals lay the blame upon the gods,
And say that evil comes from us; yet they themselves,
By their own reckless folly, suffer woes beyond
What Fate has spun for them. Even now Aegisthus,
Beyond his portion, married the wedded wife
Of Atreus’ son, and killed him on his homeward way,
Though he knew the steep destruction that awaited him.
For we had warned him, sending Hermes, keen-eyed Slayer
Of Argus, as a messenger: ‘Kill not the king,
Nor seek to woo his wife; for from Orestes shall come
The vengeance for the son of Atreus, when he reaches
The strength of youth and longs to see his native land.’
So Hermes spoke; but Aegisthus would not turn his mind
To wise counsel; and now, all at once, he has paid for all.”

Then the gray-eyed goddess Athena answered him:
“Our father, Son of Cronus, highest of rulers,
He lies in a death well fitted to his deeds;
So may every other man perish who should do likewise!
But for wise Odysseus my heart is divided,
That unhappy man, who for a long time now suffers
His sorrows far from those he loves, upon an island
Ringed round with water, where the sea has its navel.”

## Decision log

- Preserve the Ethiopians as a geographically divided people, with Poseidon
  present at a feast between the setting and rising Sun.
- Keep Zeus’s argument about mortal responsibility tied to Aegisthus’ specific
  acts, rather than turning it into an abstract maxim about fate and freedom.
- Retain Hermes’ warning in direct speech, including the prohibition against
  killing Agamemnon or courting his wife and the promised Orestes vengeance.
- Preserve Athena’s tonal turn from approving Aegisthus’ punishment to her
  divided heart for Odysseus, whose isolation is figured as the sea’s navel.
- Continue the working register as Longfellow-inspired blank verse: ceremonial
  cadence and elevated diction, with syntax allowed to remain plainly narrative.
