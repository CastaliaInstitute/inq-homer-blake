# Odyssey Book 8 — read-aloud and fidelity review

**Review status:** translator self-review; independent reader still required

## Findings

The earlier manuscript compressed and reordered the book's major performances.
The revised pass restores the opening Achilles–Odysseus quarrel song before the
games, then places the Ares–Aphrodite song during the dancing, and reserves the
wooden-horse song for the later feast and Alcinous's recognition of Odysseus's
grief.

Factual anchors restored from the Greek collation include the fifty-two young
rowers, the sacrifice of twelve sheep, eight boars, and two oxen, Demodocus's
blindness and gifted song, the named contest winners, and Euryalus's apology and
gift. The revision keeps Odysseus's tears private except to Alcinous and preserves
the book's turn into first-person autobiography.

## Beauty and cadence

The sequence now has a stronger Longfellow-like architecture: public ceremony,
competitive insult, lyric interlude, epic memory, and the quiet invitation to
confession. The recurring “song / tears / silence” movement should be tested in
the two-column proof for breath and repetition.

## Open decisions

- Independently verify the contest names and gift inventory against the full Greek.
- Check the compressed wooden-horse narrative for omitted images before approval.
- Commission an independent read-aloud and omissions/additions report.

**Conclusion:** song order and principal factual anchors corrected; not approved
for layout or final publication.
