**Method:** The English was checked against the locked Greek XML by numbered line range. The disguise and agency of Iris, the textile image, the elders’ speech, each identification, Antenor’s comparative memory, and the Dioscuri passage were checked individually.

## Decisions recorded

- **121–145 — Iris and Helen:** Restored Iris’s likeness to Laodice, Helen’s purple double-folded textile, its embroidered Trojan/Achaean labors, the silent armies, the proposed single combat, and Helen’s tears and two attendants, Aethra and Clymene.
- **146–170 — Trojan elders and Priam:** Restored the named elders, the cicada image, their qualified recognition of Helen’s beauty and wish for her return, and Priam’s explicit release of Helen from blame.
- **171–190 — Agamemnon:** Preserved Helen’s self-condemnation, the kin and child she left, Agamemnon’s double status as king and spearman, and Priam’s remembered comparison with the Phrygian and Amazon forces.
- **191–224 — Odysseus and Antenor:** Restored the ram comparison, Odysseus’s Ithacan identity and counsel, Antenor’s embassy memory, the contrast between Menelaus’s body and Odysseus’s authority, and the snow simile for Odysseus’s speech.
- **225–244 — Ajax, Idomeneus, and the Dioscuri:** Restored Ajax as the Achaean bulwark, Idomeneus among the Cretans, Menelaus’s hospitality, Helen’s inability to see Castor and Polydeuces, and the narrator’s confirmation that the earth already held them in Lacedaemon.
