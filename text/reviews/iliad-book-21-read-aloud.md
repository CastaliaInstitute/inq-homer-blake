# Iliad Book 21 — read-aloud and fidelity revision log

**Scope:** Iliad 21.1–611, current working translation  
**Date:** 2026-09-03  
**Status:** editorial working evidence; not a gate pass  
**Reviewer:** AI-assisted draft review; human sign-off required

This pass tests the river massacre, Lycaon and Asteropaeus, the River’s assault,
the fire-and-water contest, the divine quarrel, and the rout toward Troy. It
checks the Longfellow-inspired cadence against the Greek-collation units; it does
not certify meter or replace an independent omissions/additions review.

## Critical craft notes

- The strongest Longfellow-like music comes when the sentence is allowed to carry
  across the line break: the River’s channels, the elm’s fall, and Achilles’s
  prayer all need a measured forward pull rather than clipped heroic slogans.
- Repeated epithets are retained as structural music, but the translation varies
  connective syntax where repetition would flatten the River’s changing agency.
- The violence is intentionally not beautified; the surrounding similes make its
  scale visible while the Lycaon scene keeps its moral cost in the foreground.
- The current meter report remains a syllable screen. Proper names, compounds,
  and the divine speeches require a named human read-aloud editor before the
  Verse gate can pass.
