## Revised translation pass

Then Eurymachus, son of Polybus, answered him:

“Old man, go home and prophesy to thy own children,
Lest they suffer some evil afterward. In this matter
I am far better than thou at prophecy. Many birds wander
Beneath the rays of the sun, and not all are signs of good;
But Odysseus has perished far away. Would that thou too
Hadst perished with him! Then thou wouldst not speak
So many prophecies, nor stir Telemachus to anger,
Hoping perhaps that he may send a gift to thy house,
If he should give it.

“Yet I shall tell thee this, and it shall be accomplished:
If thou, knowing much of old things, lead a younger man astray
With words, and urge him to grow angry, it will first be
Most painful for him; and he will still be unable to accomplish
Anything on account of these things. But for thee, old man,
We shall impose a swift punishment, which thou shalt carry
Within thy heart; and the pain will be hard for thee.

“And I myself will advise Telemachus before them all:
Let him command his mother to return to her father.
They will make the wedding and prepare an abundant bride-price,
All that should follow a beloved daughter.
For I do not think the sons of the Achaeans will cease
From the grievous courtship, since we fear no one—
Neither Telemachus, though he speaks at length,
Nor the prophecy which thou, old man, declarest;
Rather we hate it all the more.
And his possessions shall be consumed shamefully,
Nor shall they ever be equal again, as long as she delays
The Achaeans by remaining with her marriage undecided.
We shall contend each day for her excellence,
Waiting on her, and we shall not go to other women,
Though it would be fitting for each man to marry.”
