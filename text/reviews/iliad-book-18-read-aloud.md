# Iliad Book 18 — read-aloud and fidelity revision log

**Scope:** Iliad 18.1–617, current working translation  
**Date:** 2026-09-02  
**Status:** editorial working evidence; not a gate pass  
**Reviewer:** AI-assisted draft review; human sign-off required

This pass tests Antilochus’s death-message, Achilles’ lament, Thetis’s appeal,
the Trojan council, the burial vow, Hephaestus’s forge, and the shield’s world
image. It does not certify meter or replace an independent omissions/additions
review.

## Critical notes

- Beauty depends on scale without escape: a friend’s body, a mother’s tears, a
  forge, a city court, a vineyard, a lion, a dance, and Oceanus all belong to the
  same shield, yet none cancels the grief that called it into being.
- The Longfellow-inspired register should give the shield’s catalogue a spacious,
  processional music while keeping each crafted object and civic scene concrete.
  The lament should remain more broken and bodily than the forge description.
- The meter report is only a syllable screen. A named human reader must still
  review stress, proper names, the shield’s ordered tableaux, craft vocabulary,
  and the breath of the long catalogue.
