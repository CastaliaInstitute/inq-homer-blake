## Decisions

- “I would rather keep my people safe than see them die” restores Agamemnon's
  stated motive for returning Chryseis before he asks for compensation.
- The comparison with Clytemnestra is retained as a deliberately disturbing
  measure of Agamemnon's preference; it is not softened into a generic claim
  about beauty.
- “Prize” and “honor” remain separate. The prize is material and distributable;
  honor is the public standing Agamemnon believes its loss would signify.
- Achilles's catalogue of non-injury—cattle, horses, and Phthian harvest—is
  retained to preserve the argument that his service is elective rather than
  a response to a private grievance.
- “Dog-face” retains *kunōpa* as an insult; “curved ships” retains the concrete
  Homeric image rather than normalizing it to “ships.”
- Agamemnon's logistical order (ship, rowers, hecatomb, girl, and leader) is
  restored before the later personal threat to take Briseis.

This is a working collation, not a final approval record. Apparatus consultation
