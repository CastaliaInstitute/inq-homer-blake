## Revised translation pass

“A wooded island, where the goddess dwells within her halls—
The daughter of ruinous-minded Atlas, who knows
The depths of every sea, and holds himself the pillars long
That keep the earth and heaven divided. His daughter holds
The unhappy man, lamenting, and with soft, beguiling words
Charms him, hoping he may forget the land of Ithaca.
Yet Odysseus, yearning even to see the smoke
Leap upward from his own country, desires to die.
And thy dear heart is not moved, Olympian Zeus.
Was not Odysseus gracious to thee beside the Argive ships,
Offering sacred rites in broad-soiled Troy? Why then, O Zeus,
Hast thou conceived such anger against him?”

Cloud-gathering Zeus answered her: “My child, what word
Has escaped the barrier of thy teeth? How could I forget
Godlike Odysseus, who surpasses mortal men in counsel,
And who gave offerings beyond others to the immortal gods
Who possess the broad heaven? But Earth-shaking Poseidon
Nurses his relentless anger against him for the Cyclops,
Whose eye he blinded—godlike Polyphemus, strongest
Of all the Cyclopes. The nymph Thoosa bore him,
Daughter of Phorcys, lord of the barren sea,
After she had joined with Poseidon in the hollow caves.
Therefore Poseidon, shaker of the earth, does not kill Odysseus;
He drives him wandering far from his native land.

“Come, then: let all of us here devise his return,
That he may reach his home; and Poseidon shall let go
His anger. He cannot stand alone against all the immortals,
And strive against them, unwilling, by himself.”

Then the gray-eyed goddess Athena answered him:
“Our father, Son of Cronus, highest of rulers, if now
This is indeed pleasing to the blessed gods, that wise Odysseus
Should return to his own house, let us send Hermes,
The messenger, the clear-eyed Slayer of Argus, to Ogygia,
That he may quickly declare to the fair-haired nymph
The unerring will: the return of steadfast Odysseus,
And how he is to come home. I myself will go to Ithaca,
That I may urge his son still more, and lay strength within
His heart, after calling the long-haired Achaeans to assembly,
That he may forbid all the suitors who continually slaughter
His thick-fleeced sheep and horned, crooked-footed cattle.
I will send him to Sparta and sandy Pylos,
To seek news of his dear father’s return, if perhaps he may hear it,
And that he may win a good name among mankind.”

Having spoken, she bound beneath her feet the beautiful sandals,
Golden and immortal, which carried her over wet sea and boundless land,
Together with the breath of wind. She took a mighty spear,
Sharp with its keen bronze, heavy, great, and strong,
With which she subdues the ranks of men in battle.

## Decision log

- Preserve Calypso’s lineage and Atlas’s cosmic labor, including the pillars
  that hold earth and heaven apart; the island is not merely a scenic retreat.
- Keep Odysseus’s desire to see the smoke of Ithaca and his willingness to die,
  setting his homeward longing against Calypso’s persuasive speech.
- Retain Zeus’s defense of Odysseus’s piety and Poseidon’s precise grievance:
  the blinded Cyclops is Polyphemus, son of Thoosa and Poseidon.
- Preserve Zeus’s assertion that Poseidon cannot oppose all immortals alone;
  the return is a divine consensus with one god’s anger still unresolved.
- Keep Athena’s two-part plan: Hermes to Ogygia, and Athena to Ithaca to awaken
  Telemachus, summon the assembly, confront the suitors, and seek news in Sparta
  and sandy Pylos.
- Retain Athena’s transformation into a martial traveler through the golden
  sandals and heavy spear; the next unit continues her descent to Ithaca.
