# Iliad Book 11 — read-aloud and fidelity revision log

**Scope:** Iliad 11.1–848, current working translation  
**Date:** 2026-09-02  
**Status:** editorial working evidence; not a gate pass  
**Reviewer:** AI-assisted draft review; human sign-off required

This pass tests Agamemnon’s aristeia, the commanders’ wounds, the withdrawal
around Ajax, and the Patroclus–Eurypylus ending. The book moves from bright
metallic catalogue to bodily pain and medicinal care; this log does not certify
meter or replace an independent omissions/additions review.

## Findings and actions

| Passage | Finding | Action or decision |
|---|---|---|
| 11.1–68 | Dawn, Strife, and the arming catalogue establish a hard, public battlefield register. | Preserve the Cinyras breastplate, Gorgon and Deimos/Phobos imagery, the serpent motif, and the contrast between empty chariots and living drivers. |
| 11.69–180 | Agamemnon’s advance rises through named kills, then turns abruptly when Coon wounds him. | Retain Iphidamas’s marriage-price, Coon’s grief and ambush, Agamemnon’s wound, and Zeus’s timed instruction to Hector. |
| 11.181–253 | Hector’s counterstroke, Diomedes’ resistance, and Paris’s arrow create a chain of distinct agencies. | Keep the Merops sons, Agastrophus, Apollo’s protection of Hector, Paris’s shot at Diomedes’ foot, and the precise withdrawal. |
| 11.254–344 | Odysseus’s isolation and rescue by Menelaus and Ajax should remain audible as tactical crisis. | Preserve Socus’s shield-and-breastplate wound, the triple cry, the wolf/stag and lion/cub images, and Ajax’s river-like pursuit. |
| 11.345–482 | The lineation shifts into blank verse as Machaon is wounded and Ajax is forced back. | Keep Cebriones’ advice, Zeus’s sudden fear, the ass simile, Eurypylus’s counterstroke, and the distinction between standing wounded and fleeing. |
| 11.483–617 | Nestor’s kykeon and Pylian exemplum convert battlefield urgency into inherited counsel. | Preserve Hecamede, the four-eared cup, the Pramnian wine, the Epean raid, the Moliones, and Menoetius’s charge to Patroclus. |
| 11.618–848 | Patroclus’s pity begins the chain of borrowed armor and future catastrophe. | Retain Eurypylus’s request, Chiron’s medicines, the wounded physicians, the arrow extraction, and the unresolved pain at the close. |

## Critical notes

- Beauty is strongest where the book changes texture: bronze and blood give way
  to sweat, barley, warm water, roots, and a wound that dries without ceasing to
  hurt. The final copyedit should preserve that sensory descent.
- The Longfellow-inspired register should keep the catalogue stately while making
  each agency unmistakable; repeated epithets must not obscure who strikes,
  withdraws, counsels, or heals.
- The opening Dawn, arming catalogue, Trojan mustering, and first Agamemnon
  advance were recast into sustained verse in this pass. The meter report is
  only a syllable screen. A named human reader must still
  review stress, proper names, catalogue breath, and the transition into the
  newly recast blank-verse ending.
