# Illustration art direction: the five-plate return arc

**Status:** development brief; prompt-ready placeholders only  
**Date:** 2026-09-02  
**Scope:** five original supplemental plate concepts for the two-volume Homer
edition

This brief turns five existing book-slot placeholders into a coherent priority
set. It does not commission, generate, select, or approve artwork. No raster
asset is associated with these records, and none may be treated as a print
master.

## Attribution and provenance boundary

The visual premise is Blake-informed, not Blake-authored: visionary intensity,
energetic contour, symbolic compression, and illuminated contrast may guide the
new work, but no prompt may imitate, trace, reconstruct, or quote a specific
William Blake plate. The historical precedent is the 1805 Flaxman Homer
edition, in which John Flaxman is the designer and William Blake is one of the
engravers; a Flaxman composition must not be captioned as a Blake composition.

Each new image described here is an original CastaliaInstitute supplement. If
one is eventually commissioned or generated, its record must use the credit
line:

> Original plate by CastaliaInstitute; not by William Blake.

The final handoff must add the responsible illustrator or production system,
creation date, source/prompt record, checksum, dimensions, profile, rights
status, exact collated passage, caption, and final file to the appropriate
manifest. Until those fields exist, status remains
`placeholder / prompt-ready / not for print`.

## Shared visual grammar

The five plates should read as one family while allowing each epic a distinct
temperature.

- **Format:** 7 × 10 inch portrait, with a quiet outer margin for caption,
  folio, trim, and any printer-required bleed. Compose for legibility at final
  placement size, not for detail that disappears when reduced.
- **Mark:** original etched contour, relief-print pressure, handmade line
  variation, selective hatching, and broad paper-white intervals. Anatomy is
  elongated and expressive but physically plausible; gesture carries the
  emotion.
- **Palette:** warm paper white, carbon black, smoke gray, and one restrained
  accent wash. Iliad plates may lean toward iron, ash, and forge-gold; Odyssey
  plates may lean toward sea-indigo, olive, and dawn-gold. Do not let color
  become a glossy digital effect.
- **Space:** compressed architecture or landscape, with one readable primary
  action and no crowded anecdotal background. Use a strong vertical, diagonal,
  or rooted axis so the image remains legible beside two-column text.
- **Recurring symbols:** borrowed armor or clothing, a threshold, a hand that
  withholds or recognizes, and an olive/root form. These are visual rhymes, not
  literal icon sets; each plate must remain faithful to its scene.
- **Prohibitions:** no text, lettering, border, watermark, signature, publisher
  mark, modern object, fantasy armor, cinematic photorealism, pin-up framing,
  gratuitous gore, or anachronistic anatomy. Do not place critical detail in
  the gutter or bleed zone.

## Concept records

### ILI-16-PATROCLUS — the borrowed sun

- **Book / provisional anchor:** *Iliad* 16; Patroclus takes Achilles' armor,
  drives the Trojans from the ships, and moves toward his fall. Exact Greek
  and English range to lock after editorial collation.
- **Narrative function:** Make the danger of substitution visible. Patroclus
  is radiant because he carries another man's signs, yet the composition must
  show the armor's identity beginning to exceed the wearer.
- **Composition:** A tall central figure advances from the ships toward Troy.
  The borrowed helmet and shield create a hard luminous frame around Patroclus,
  while a smaller, darker echo of Achilles remains at the tent or ship-line
  behind him. Sarpedon's fall may be suggested as a low broken diagonal, but
  the focal event is Patroclus crossing the limit of the role.
- **Prompt:**

  > Create an original 7 × 10 inch portrait plate for Homer’s *Iliad*, Book 16,
  > for CastaliaInstitute. Show Patroclus entering battle in Achilles’ borrowed
  > armor, leaving the dark ships behind and moving toward the distant Trojan
  > wall. Let the helmet, shield, and spear form a bright vertical axis while
  > the human figure beneath them remains vulnerable and distinct. Suggest the
  > fall of Sarpedon and the approaching catastrophe through a low diagonal of
  > dust, shadow, and broken standards, without graphic violence. Use original
  > etched black line, relief-print pressure, warm paper white, smoke gray,
  > iron-black, and one restrained forge-gold wash; elongated but credible
  > gesture, compressed epic space, strong light/dark structure, and quiet
  > caption margins. The visual energy may be informed by Blake’s visionary
  > contour and symbolic intensity, but this must be a new composition and must
  > not imitate, trace, reconstruct, or quote any specific Blake plate. No text,
  > border, watermark, signature, modern object, fantasy armor, photorealism,
  > gore, or anachronistic anatomy.
- **Editorial check:** Confirm that the selected range contains both the armor's
  transformation of the battle and Patroclus' overreach; do not caption a
  Sarpedon-only scene as the whole concept.
- **Handoff state:** `placeholder / prompt-ready / not for print`; credit as an
  original CastaliaInstitute plate if produced.

### ILI-24-PRIAM — the lamp between enemies

- **Book / provisional anchor:** *Iliad* 24; Priam enters Achilles' tent and
  receives Hector's body. Exact range to lock after editorial collation.
- **Narrative function:** End the Iliad visual sequence in a small room where
  pity is more powerful than battle scale. The plate should be spacious and
  unsensational, with the fathers' shared grief carrying the image.
- **Composition:** Priam kneels or bends at the left, Achilles sits at the
  right, and a small lamp or brazier occupies the open center. Hector's body is
  present beyond the tent opening only as a covered, shadowed form; never make
  the corpse the spectacle. The tent poles create a temporary architecture of
  equal verticals, while the night outside keeps Troy and the war at a
  distance.
- **Prompt:**

  > Create an original 7 × 10 inch portrait plate for Homer’s *Iliad*, Book 24,
  > showing aged Priam entering Achilles’ tent to ask for Hector’s body. Place
  > Priam and Achilles as two grave, nearly equal vertical presences across a
  > small shared lamp; let the lamp illuminate hands, faces, and the space
  > between enemies. Keep Hector’s covered body outside the tent in deep shadow,
  > present but not displayed. Use original black etched contour, broad paper
  > white, ash gray, muted iron, and a single dim amber-gold light; spare
  > architecture, elongated expressive gesture, quiet negative space, and
  > compassionate severity. The visual energy may be informed by Blake’s
  > visionary line and moral compression, but do not imitate, trace, reconstruct,
  > or quote any specific Blake plate. No text, border, watermark, signature,
  > modern objects, fantasy armor, photorealism, gore, or melodrama.
- **Editorial check:** Confirm the caption names Priam's request and Achilles'
  response, not a generic “Hector's funeral” scene. Keep the encounter
  interior and human-scale.
- **Handoff state:** `placeholder / prompt-ready / not for print`; credit as an
  original CastaliaInstitute plate if produced.

### ODY-13-RETURN — the veiled harbor

- **Book / provisional anchor:** *Odyssey* 13; Odysseus lands in Ithaca,
  Athena reveals the island and disguises him, and they conceal the gifts in
  the nymphs' cave. Exact range to lock after editorial collation.
- **Narrative function:** Mark the change from wandering to strategy. Ithaca
  is home, but it is first encountered as an obscured, almost unreadable place;
  the return is a threshold, not a triumphant arrival.
- **Composition:** Put the sleeping or newly awakened Odysseus low in the
  foreground by Phorcys harbor. Athena, first as a shepherd-like stranger and
  then suggested through a second, more radiant contour, governs the center.
  The olive tree and cave entrance should anchor the lower third; the sea-road
  should recede behind them rather than dominate. Make the transformation read
  as a veil of line and posture, not magical particles.
- **Prompt:**

  > Create an original 7 × 10 inch portrait plate for Homer’s *Odyssey*, Book
  > 13, showing Odysseus newly landed at Ithaca as Athena reveals the harbor,
  > olive tree, and cave of the nymphs before disguising him for the next stage
  > of his return. Compose a veiled harbor in dawn-dark light: Odysseus low and
  > vulnerable near the shore, Athena at the center in a shepherd’s plain
  > guise, with a second contour of her divine authority implied by posture and
  > light rather than fantasy effects. Let the olive and cave form a stable
  > rooted axis, while the sea recedes into a narrow opening behind them. Use
  > original etched black line, warm paper white, smoke gray, muted sea-indigo,
  > and a restrained olive-gold wash; compressed space, intelligent negative
  > space, readable silhouette, and quiet caption margins. The direction may be
  > informed by Blake’s visionary intensity but must not imitate, trace,
  > reconstruct, or quote any specific Blake plate. No text, border, watermark,
  > signature, modern objects, fantasy armor, photorealism, gore, or glittering
  > magical effects.
- **Editorial check:** Keep the action within Book 13. Do not import Eumaeus'
  hut, the swineherd's dogs, or the meal from Book 14 into this plate.
- **Handoff state:** `placeholder / prompt-ready / not for print`; credit as an
  original CastaliaInstitute plate if produced.

### ODY-16-RECOGNITION — the doubled flame

- **Book / provisional anchor:** *Odyssey* 16; Telemachus reaches Eumaeus'
  hut, Athena restores Odysseus' appearance, and father and son recognize one
  another. Exact range to lock after editorial collation.
- **Narrative function:** Give the Odyssey a human counterpart to the Iliad's
  borrowed armor: disguise is removed through relationship, not spectacle.
- **Composition:** Build the image around the low hearth. Telemachus and
  Odysseus face one another across it, with Eumaeus turned slightly away at the
  edge so the private recognition remains central. A single flame should divide
  and join them; the hut roof and olive branches make a protective, temporary
  geometry, while the road outside remains visible but secondary.
- **Prompt:**

  > Create an original 7 × 10 inch portrait plate for Homer’s *Odyssey*, Book
  > 16, showing Telemachus recognizing Odysseus in Eumaeus’ hut after Athena
  > restores the father's appearance. Center the composition on a low hearth:
  > father and son face one another across one doubled flame, their hands and
  > postures carrying the shock of recognition without theatrical embrace.
  > Place the loyal swineherd at the edge of the scene, turned respectfully
  > aside, and let the hut, olive branches, and narrow road create a humble
  > shelter around the encounter. Use original etched black contour, warm paper
  > white, smoke gray, muted olive, and a restrained dawn-gold wash; intimate
  > compressed architecture, credible anatomy, strong silhouette, and generous
  > caption margins. The work may be informed by Blake’s visionary contour but
  > must not imitate, trace, reconstruct, or quote any specific Blake plate. No
  > text, border, watermark, signature, modern objects, fantasy armor,
  > photorealism, gore, or sentimental greeting-card sentiment.
- **Editorial check:** The plate must show the revelation in the hut, not the
  later battle plan or the arrival in the palace. Preserve Eumaeus' presence
  without making him a third protagonist.
- **Handoff state:** `placeholder / prompt-ready / not for print`; credit as an
  original CastaliaInstitute plate if produced.

### ODY-23-BED — the living root

- **Book / provisional anchor:** *Odyssey* 23; Penelope tests the returned
  Odysseus by speaking of the bed, and the rooted olive proves his identity.
  Exact range to lock after editorial collation.
- **Narrative function:** Close the priority arc with a proof that cannot be
  moved. The visual answer to the veiled harbor is an interior whose deepest
  architecture is alive.
- **Composition:** Show the bed as a living olive trunk rising through the
  chamber, not as a decorative bedpost. Penelope stands in measured distance,
  testing; Odysseus answers with knowledge of the rooted construction. The
  room's beams and the tree's branches should share one structural rhythm. Keep
  the embrace deferred or quiet, so recognition comes from the fact of the
  house.
- **Prompt:**

  > Create an original 7 × 10 inch portrait plate for Homer’s *Odyssey*, Book
  > 23, showing Penelope testing the returned Odysseus through the secret of the
  > rooted bed. Make a living olive trunk rise from the floor through the bed's
  > construction and continue into the chamber's beam-like geometry, physically
  > grounded rather than ornamental. Place Penelope in a composed, questioning
  > stance and Odysseus opposite her, answering through recognition of the
  > immovable root; let any embrace remain quiet and secondary. Use original
  > etched black line, warm paper white, smoke gray, deep olive, and a restrained
  > moon-gold wash; intimate night architecture, symbolic clarity, elongated
  > gesture, and quiet caption margins. The direction may be informed by Blake’s
  > visionary intensity but must not imitate, trace, reconstruct, or quote any
  > specific Blake plate. No text, border, watermark, signature, modern
  > objects, fantasy architecture, photorealism, gore, or sentimental wedding
  > imagery.
- **Editorial check:** Verify that the image makes the bed's rooted olive
  construction legible. Do not substitute a generic reunion, a free-standing
  olive tree, or a palace exterior.
- **Handoff state:** `placeholder / prompt-ready / not for print`; credit as an
  original CastaliaInstitute plate if produced.

## Review sequence before any image generation or commission

1. Lock the exact Greek/English passage for the selected concept and record the
   range in the illustration manifest.
2. Test the prompt as a grayscale thumbnail at 7 × 10 inches. Reject any
   composition whose primary action is not legible beside the two-column text.
3. Check the image for accidental quotation of a historical Blake/Flaxman
   plate, false archaeological specificity, or visual clichés that weaken the
   scene.
4. Record the production source, prompt version, checksum, dimensions, profile,
   rights, caption, and credit before considering a candidate for curation.
5. Keep the state `concept-review` until human editorial and art-direction
   approval, native-resolution preparation, color conversion, and physical
   proofing are complete.
