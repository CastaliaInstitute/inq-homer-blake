## Decisions

- Thersites's physical description is retained as hostile narratorial rhetoric,
  while his political complaint is allowed to remain intelligible rather than
  being reduced to mere buffoonery.
- Odysseus's violence is shown in sequence: threat, blow, welt, tears, and the
  army's laughter. The translation does not recast coercion as uncomplicated
  communal agreement.
- The omen preserves the count of eight chicks and one mother, the stone
  transformation, and Calchas's nine-year/tenth-year interpretation.
- The Aulis sacrifice and Zeus's favorable lightning on the right remain the
  evidence Nestor uses to oppose premature flight.
- “Sort the men by tribes and clans” preserves the practical military purpose of
  *phylai* and *phrētrai*: units should support one another and make leadership
  visible in battle.
- Agamemnon's closing order keeps the physical cost of battle—sweating straps,
  tiring hands, and sweating horses—before the threat to deserters.

This is a working collation, not a final approval record. Apparatus consultation
