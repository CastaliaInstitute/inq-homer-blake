**Report date:** 2026-09-03

## Editorial conclusion

The illustration system has 48 defined book slots and 30 current manifest
records. They cover 23 unique book slots with original generated candidates:
14 Iliad and 9 Odyssey. The current set is useful for editorial selection and
art-direction review, but it contains no approved final art. Two records are
historical references from the 1805 Flaxman Homer edition; the other 28 are
CastaliaInstitute original generated concepts, including alternates. The historical material must be presented as
Flaxman-designed work with the recorded engraver, never as Blake compositions.
The original concepts may be Blake-informed in visual language, but they are not
William Blake works and must retain the credit boundary “Original plate by
CastaliaInstitute; not by William Blake.”

The 3 September production pass added new, checksum-bound, book-owned candidates
for ILI-04, ILI-05, ILI-06, ILI-07, ILI-08, ILI-09, ILI-10, and ILI-12. Each
has an exact whole-book source anchor, prompt record, contemporary-work credit,
and a portrait source image whose color profile is explicitly recorded. They remain concept-review and require
print-master upscaling, art-direction approval, and physical proofing.

The strongest immediate editorial arc is: Iliad 1, 2, 3, 11, 16, 24 and
Odyssey 1, 2, 5, 9, 22. It gives both volumes an opening, threshold, crisis,
and return/reclamation rhythm. The five-plate return arc in the art-direction
brief—ILI-16, ILI-24, ODY-13, ODY-16, ODY-23—should remain the next original
commissioning brief. All five slots now have concept candidates; ODY-13 is
represented by a new original concept and remains subject to the same human
art-direction and print gates. Historical references should be retained as
contextual options, not used to imply that the 48-slot program is historically
illustrated.

### What the images currently demonstrate

The generated set already has a recognizable family: portrait compositions,
etched or engraved-looking contours, paper-colored grounds, compressed space,
and restrained accent washes. The strongest recurring structures are thresholds,
vertical divine figures, storm/sea diagonals, intimate tent or hut interiors,
and the contrast between a small human figure and an elemental field. These are
appropriate as direction and selection evidence. They are not evidence that a
final plate has been produced, that a rights clearance exists, or that a
particular Homer passage has been visually proven.

## Provenance and print-readiness risks

1. **Historical attribution risk.** The Met record supports John Flaxman as
   designer and identifies the engraver plate by plate. It does not support
   calling either scan a Blake composition. The former
   `iliad-book-01-flaxman-blake` identifier was especially easy to misread and
   has been replaced by the explicit
   `iliad-book-01-minerva-flaxman-design-blake-engraving` identifier across the
   manifest and checksum ledger.
2. **Concept/final confusion.** Every generated manifest row is
   `concept-review`; the art-direction brief explicitly says the concepts are
   placeholders and not for print. The report therefore recommends selection
   for the next review round, not approval.
3. **Resolution and derivative risk.** Most generated source assets are about
   150–170 dpi at the working placement scale, and three manifest entries use
   2100 × 3000 placement enlargements. An enlarged placement file is not a
   native print master. The two native 2100 × 3000 v2 assets are better starting
   points, but still require profile conversion and proofing.
4. **Color/profile risk.** Historical scans are recorded as sRGB; generated
   assets are `unprofiled`. The target stock and printer profile are specified,
   but no generated plate has completed conversion, embedded profile check, or
   physical proof.
5. **Passage and caption risk.** Most current rows use whole-book ranges or
   provisional anchors. Exact Greek/English ranges, captions, and primary
   actions must be line-collated before an image is treated as editorially
   final. ILI-24 is an explicit caption mismatch to resolve.
6. **Manifest integrity risk.** The audit found ODY-22 twice with the same
   asset, checksum, and prompt; the duplicate record has now been removed and
   the asset preflight will reject an identical duplicate in future.
7. **Rights-record gap.** “Original concept; not by William Blake” is an
   attribution boundary, not a complete rights clearance or license statement.
   The responsible production system, applicable institutional policy, and
   final rights status must be recorded without inference before release.

## Required gate before any slot becomes final

For each selected candidate, lock the exact Greek/English passage and caption;
confirm the primary action and trim-safe composition at 6.625 × 10.25 inches;
retain the source asset, prompt or source record, checksum, creator/production
record, provenance URL or historical object record, rights status, dimensions,
embedded printer color profile, and final asset path; then complete human
editorial/art-direction approval and physical or printer proofing.

Until every applicable gate is complete, the selected material must continue to
be labeled **concept-review / not for print**. In particular, concept-review
assets are not final art.
