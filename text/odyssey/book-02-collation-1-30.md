## Revised translation pass

When early-born, rose-fingered Dawn appeared,
The dear son of Odysseus rose from his bed.
He dressed himself, and laid the sharp sword at his shoulder;
He bound the beautiful sandals beneath his shining feet,
And went out from his chamber, face to face like a god.

At once he commanded the clear-voiced heralds
To summon the long-haired Achaeans to assembly.
They proclaimed it; and the people gathered very swiftly.
When they had assembled and were gathered together,
Telemachus went toward the place of assembly, holding
The bronze spear in his hand—not alone, for two white hounds
Followed him. Athena poured a wondrous grace around him,
And all the people gazed upon him as he approached.
He sat down in his father’s seat, and the elders made room.

Then the hero Aegyptius began to speak among them.
Age had bent him, but he knew countless things;
For his dear son Antiphus had gone with godlike Odysseus
In the hollow ships to horse-pastured Ilium—
Antiphus the spearman, whom the savage Cyclops killed
In his hollow cave, and made his final meal.
Three other sons remained: Eurynomus joined the suitors,
While two kept always to the work inherited from their father.
Yet even so he had not forgotten him in grief and pain;
He shed his tears, and spoke among the people:

“Now hear me, Ithacans, while I say what I must say.”

## Decision log

- Preserve Telemachus’ dawn transformation through clothing, sword, sandals,
  and the godlike public appearance that begins Book 2’s civic movement.
- Keep the heralds’ summons and the assembly’s quick gathering as formal acts,
  not background exposition; the long-haired Achaeans answer a public call.
- Retain Athena’s grace, the two white hounds, the bronze spear, and the elders’
  yielding of Odysseus’ seat as visible signs of inherited authority.
- Preserve Aegyptius’ bent age and his family history: Antiphus died in the
  Cyclops’ cave, Eurynomus joined the suitors, and two sons kept the father’s work.
- End at the source’s opening of Aegyptius’ speech on line 30; his question about
  who called the assembly continues in the next source-defined unit.
