# Odyssey Book 22 — battle and judgment review

**Review status:** translator self-review; independent reader still required

## Findings

The manuscript preserves the battle’s staged disclosure: Antinous dies before
the name is fully declared, Eurymachus’s settlement is rejected, the first
counterattack arms Telemachus and the loyal herdsmen, and the storeroom breach
creates the tactical crisis. Melanthius’s binding and hanging, Athena’s Mentor
form, the aegis, and the named deaths remain distinct steps.

Leiodes’s plea is restored before Phemius and Medon are spared. This distinction
matters: Leiodes participated in the suitors’ feast and is judged with them;
Phemius’s compelled song and Medon’s care for Telemachus are testified to by
the prince, and both find refuge at the altar.

The closing keeps the disloyal women’s hanging, Melanthius’s mutilation, sulfur
purification, and the faithful women’s recognition within Book 22, while leaving
Penelope’s direct recognition to Book 23.

## Beauty and cadence

The hall should move from the clean twang of the bow to blunt bodily violence,
then into the suspended quiet of the altar. The fish-on-the-shore simile gives
the massacre a cold public image; the later cleaning and fire return the scene
to household labor, but without erasing what the household has witnessed.

## Open decisions

- Independently verify the named deaths, armory quantities, and Melanthius’s two
  punishments against the full Greek.
- Check the agency and testimony in the Leiodes, Phemius, and Medon episodes.
- Commission an independent read-aloud and omissions/additions report.

**Conclusion:** battle and judgment sequence restored; not approved for layout
or final publication.
