# Iliad Book 17 — read-aloud and fidelity revision log

**Scope:** Iliad 17.1–761, current working translation  
**Date:** 2026-09-02  
**Status:** editorial working evidence; not a gate pass  
**Reviewer:** AI-assisted draft review; human sign-off required

This pass tests the struggle over Patroclus’s body, the stripping and wearing of
Achilles’ armor, the alternating recoveries, the grief of Achilles’ horses, and
the final coordinated withdrawal. It does not certify meter or replace an
independent omissions/additions review.

## Critical notes

- Beauty depends on custody: the body, armor, horses, and message pass from hand
  to hand, but none passes cleanly. Keep the repeated verbs of lifting, dragging,
  stripping, guarding, and carrying audible.
- The Longfellow-inspired register should make the grief ceremonious without
  slowing the tactical line. The alternation of long similes and short combat
  clauses is the book’s principal breath pattern.
- The meter report is only a syllable screen. A named human reader must still
  review stress, proper names, divine disguises, armor terminology, and the
  transitions between battlefield and lament.
