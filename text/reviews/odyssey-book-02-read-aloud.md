# Odyssey Book 2 — read-aloud revision log

**Scope:** Odyssey 2.1–434, current working translation  
**Date:** 2026-09-03  
**Status:** editorial working evidence; not a gate pass  
**Reviewer:** AI-assisted draft review; human sign-off required

This pass checks the book's public assembly, Penelope's weaving account, the
eagle omen, and Telemachus's departure for narrative momentum, ceremonial
register, and fidelity-sensitive compression. It does not certify meter or
Greek accuracy. Independent source comparison, full stress analysis, and human
sign-off remain open.

## Findings and actions

| Passage | Finding | Action or decision |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1–30 | Telemachus's emergence must be visible before it becomes rhetorical. | Retain the dawn, sword, sandals, scepter, hounds, father's seat, and Athena's grace as a deliberate sequence of public signs. |
| 2.31–79 | Aegyptius's question establishes the assembly's broken civic continuity. | Preserve the claim that no assembly has met since Odysseus departed and let the old man's uncertainty precede Telemachus's speech; do not over-explain the constitutional stakes. |
| 2.80–128 | Telemachus's first public accusation must balance grief, legal appeal, and threat. | Keep the house's consumed livelihood, the vultures' image, the appeal to gods and neighbors, and the thrown scepter; test “private sorrow” against the source's public force. |
| 2.129–176 | Antinous's account of the shroud is both persuasive rhetoric and self-serving distortion. | Preserve the three years, the unwinding by torchlight, the fourth-year betrayal, and the demand that Penelope be sent away; avoid making the deception either comic or exculpatory. |
| 2.177–241 | The reply, eagle omen, and Halitherses's interpretation move from threatened justice to prophetic confirmation. | Keep Telemachus's refusal to expel his mother, the rightward flight, and the distinction between omen and interpretation; retain uncertainty until the speaker names its consequence. |
| 2.242–276 | Mentor's rebuke exposes the people's silence, while Leocritus normalizes violence as practical necessity. | Preserve the scepter language and the contrast between civic responsibility and fatalistic refusal; do not make the people passive by accident. |
| 2.277–327 | Athena turns private resolve into logistics: ship, crew, wine, water, secrecy, and provisions. | Keep the conditional timetable, Eurycleia's warning, the eleven-or-twelve-day secrecy, and the suitors' divinely induced sleep as distinct steps toward agency. |
| 2.328–434 | The departure must feel earned by the assembly rather than appended as adventure scenery. | Preserve Noemon's loan, the trusted companions, mast and sail, libation, following wind, and the sleeping suitors' ominous dreams; read the final starry sea as a threshold, not a conclusion. |

## Critical notes

- The book's strongest formal movement is from body to voice to vessel: first
  Telemachus is seen, then heard, then entrusted with a ship. Lineation should
  make that development audible.
- Legal and household vocabulary—assembly, scepter, livelihood, blood-price,
  winding cloth, and oath—needs a cross-book concordance so the elevated style
  remains precise rather than merely archaic.
- The machine meter report cannot judge the political speeches by syllable band
  alone. Human review should test whether the long sentences gather pressure or
  merely accumulate clauses.
