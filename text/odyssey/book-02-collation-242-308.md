## Revised translation pass

Leocritus, son of Evenor, answered him:
“Mad Mentor, what word is this with which thou urgest us
To cease? It is hard for men, and for the greater number,
To fight over a feast. Even if Odysseus himself should come
And desire within his heart to drive the noble suitors
From his own halls while they are feasting, his wife would not rejoice
At his return, much as she longs for him; rather, there he would find
A shameful death, if he fought against the many.
Thou hast not spoken according to what is fated.

“Come, let the people scatter, each to his work;
And let Mentor and Halitherses urge this man upon his road—
They are his father’s inherited friends from the beginning.
Yet I think he will sit for a long time in Ithaca waiting for news,
And he shall never make this journey.”

So he spoke, and swiftly dissolved the assembly.
The people scattered, each man toward his own house;
The suitors went into the house of godlike Odysseus.

Telemachus went apart to the shore of the sea,
Washed his hands in the gray salt water, and prayed to Athena:
“Hear me, goddess, thou who camest yesterday into our house
And commanded me to go in a ship over the misty sea,
To seek news of my father, long absent. The Achaeans delay
All these things, and most of all the suitors, violent in their pride.”

So he prayed; and Athena came near him,
In the form and voice of Mentor. She spoke winged words:
“Telemachus, thou shalt not be cowardly or foolish hereafter,
If the brave strength of thy father has been planted within thee—
Such as he was, to accomplish both word and deed.
Then thy journey shall not be empty or unfinished.
But if thou art not born of him and Penelope,
I have no hope that thou shalt accomplish what thou desirest;
For few sons are like their fathers, and most are worse,
While only a few surpass their fathers.
Yet since thou shalt not be cowardly or foolish hereafter,
And the mind of Odysseus has not wholly left thee,
There is hope that thou shalt finish these deeds.

“Therefore leave the suitors’ counsel and thought to those men
Who are senseless, since they know neither judgment nor justice;
They do not know that death and black fate stand near them,
Destined to destroy them all on a single day.
But the road thou desirest shall no longer be far away;
For I am such a father’s friend that I shall fit out
A swift ship for thee and go with thee myself.
Go now to the house and mingle with the suitors;
Prepare all provisions, and store them in vessels:
Wine in the two-handled jars, and barley meal,
The strength of men, in thick leather skins.
I shall quickly gather willing companions through the people.
There are many ships in sea-girt Ithaca, old and new;
I shall inspect them, and choose the best,
Then swiftly fit her out and launch her upon the broad sea.”

So spoke Athena, daughter of Zeus. Telemachus did not remain long,
For he had heard the voice of a god. He went toward the house,
His heart grieving, and found the proud suitors within the halls,
Some flaying goats and others roasting swine in the court.

Antinous came straight toward him, laughing, took his hand,
And spoke, calling him by name:
“Telemachus, high speaker, with your unrestrained strength,
Let no other evil deed or word trouble your heart;
But eat and drink with us, as before.
All these things the Achaeans shall accomplish for thee—
A ship and chosen rowers, that thou mayest reach holy Pylos
And hear news of thy excellent father.”
