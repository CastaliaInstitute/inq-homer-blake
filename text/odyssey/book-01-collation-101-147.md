## Revised translation pass

With which she subdues the ranks of men, the heroes
Against whom the father’s anger rises. Down from Olympus’ peak
She sprang, and stood within the people of Ithaca,
Before Odysseus’ doors, upon the outer threshold;
In her hand she held the bronze spear, and wore the likeness
Of Mentes, lord of the Taphians. There she found the proud suitors.
They sat before the doors, amusing their hearts with draughts,
Upon the hides of cattle which they themselves had slain.
Heralds and diligent serving-men attended them:
Some mixed the wine with water in the mixing-bowls;
Others, with many-pierced sponges, washed the tables
And set them forth, while the suitors divided among themselves
The abundant meat.

Godlike Telemachus saw her first. He sat among the suitors,
His heart grieving, imagining his noble father:
Whether he might come from somewhere, scatter the suitors
Throughout the house, and take the honor and kingship
Of his own halls again. Thinking thus, he saw Athena.
He went straight toward the threshold, and felt shame within him
That a stranger should stand so long before the doors.
Drawing near, he took her right hand, received her bronze spear,
And spoke winged words: “Welcome, stranger; here among us
Thou shalt be made welcome. First partake of food at supper;
Then thou shalt tell us what thy need may be.”

So speaking, he led the way, and Pallas Athena followed.
When they had entered the high-roofed house, he set her spear
Against a tall pillar within the polished spear-stand,
Where many other spears of long-enduring Odysseus stood.
He led her to a throne, and seated her upon it,
Beautifully wrought, with a footstool beneath her feet;
Then placed his own inlaid chair apart from the suitors,
Lest the stranger, disturbed by their uproar, should lose her pleasure
In the meal, surrounded by arrogant men, and lest he might ask her
About his father, who had gone away.

A serving-woman brought water in a beautiful golden pitcher,
And poured it over their hands above a silver basin;
Then she drew a polished table near. The reverend housekeeper
Brought bread and set upon it many foods, gladly offering
What was at hand. The carver lifted plates of every kind of meat
And set them near; beside them he placed golden cups;
And the herald came often, pouring wine among them.

Then the proud suitors entered. They sat down in their places,
Upon the chairs and thrones; and the heralds poured water
Over their hands, while the serving-women brought them bread
In baskets.

## Decision log

- Preserve Athena’s martial entrance and her identity as Mentes, lord of the
  Taphians; the disguise is social and political as well as visual.
- Retain the suitors’ feast as an economy of consumption: cattle they killed,
  mixed wine, washed tables, and meat divided without household restraint.
- Keep Telemachus’ first sight of Athena, his shame before the waiting stranger,
  and his welcome before any interrogation; hospitality precedes knowledge.
- Preserve the architecture of the spear-stand and the separation of Telemachus’
  chair from the suitors, making the absent father materially present.
- Retain the ordered sequence of hand-washing, table, bread, meats, cups, wine,
  and the suitors’ subsequent entrance; ritual here exposes disorder by contrast.
