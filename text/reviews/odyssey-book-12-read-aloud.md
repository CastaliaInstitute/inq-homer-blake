# Odyssey Book 12 — read-aloud and fidelity review

**Review status:** translator self-review; independent reader still required

## Findings

The manuscript preserves the causal chain from Elpenor’s burial through Circe’s
warnings, the Sirens, Scylla and Charybdis, the forbidden cattle, Zeus’s bolt,
and the solitary drift to Ogygia. The Wandering Rocks are now identified as a
distinct alternative route, with Argo as the exceptional passage Hera permitted.

The Thrinacian warning now keeps the Greek scale: seven herds of cattle and
seven flocks of sheep, fifty in each, tended by the Sun’s daughters. The moral
structure is explicit: Tiresias and Circe give the same prohibition, the crew
swears to obey it, and hunger plus Eurylochus’s argument produces the violation
that Zeus punishes.

## Source-fidelity correction recorded in this pass

The contrary-winds sentence was corrected against Odyssey 12.325–326. The
previous draft added a West Wind and repeated the South Wind; the Greek instead
states that the South Wind blew without pause and that no other wind arose
except East and South. The revised line preserves that narrower weather report.

**Pinned evidence:** Perseus `tlg0012.tlg002.perseus-grc2`, source commit

## Beauty and cadence

Circe’s speech should gather force by accumulation, while the strait should
contract into short, urgent commands. The Sirens’ promise of total knowledge
must remain alluring enough to explain Odysseus’s consent to be bound; Scylla’s
six losses should then arrive with blunt speed. The cattle episode benefits from
the longer, more mournful sentences of delayed catastrophe.
