## Decisions

- The prior opening revision restores the priest's public address rather than
  treating the ransom objects as sufficient context for his appeal.
- The public assent and Agamemnon's refusal are now present before the private
  prayer. This restores the causal hinge: the plague answers a rejected
  supplication, not an unopposed arrival.
- Agamemnon's threat retains the Greek's domestic claim on Chryseis—old age in
  Argos, work at the loom, and sharing his bed—rather than reducing her to a
  nameless captured prize.
- Chryses' fearful, silent departure along the sea is restored before the
  prayer, preserving the poem's movement from public humiliation to divine
  appeal.
- “Swift ships” keeps the formulaic *thoai nēes* concrete; “before the ships”
  is retained where the Greek marks Chryses' arrival at the beach.
- “Far-darting Apollo” preserves *hekēbolos* as a formal epithet rather than
  replacing it with a generic description of archery.
- “Covered quiver” keeps the visual detail of *amphērēphēs pharetrē* while
  avoiding an explanatory gloss inside the verse.
- “Mules and dogs … then men” preserves the poem's deliberate escalation from
  the camp's animals to its human population.
