## Decisions

- The five-year-old ox, named counselors, prayer, sacrifice, feast, and Nestor's
  order remain as a continuous ritual-to-war sequence.
- Agamemnon's prayer is not softened: he asks to burn Priam's house and tear
  Hector's shirt while his companions fall, and Zeus refuses the outcome while
  increasing the labor.
- Athena's aegis retains its hundred golden tassels, each valued at a hundred
  oxen; the image is material, political, and deliberately excessive.
- The bird, fire, fly, goat, and bull similes remain distinct stages of the
  army's movement and hierarchy rather than being compressed into “the army
  gathered.”
- The catalogue invocation keeps the Muses' epistemic role: the poet hears
  glory, while the goddesses are present, know all, and remember the names.

This is a working collation, not a final approval record. Apparatus consultation
and a second reader's sign-off are still required before the book can advance.
