# Iliad Book 12 — read-aloud and fidelity revision log

**Scope:** Iliad 12.1–471, current working translation  
**Date:** 2026-09-02  
**Status:** editorial working evidence; not a gate pass  
**Reviewer:** AI-assisted draft review; human sign-off required

This pass tests the wall’s history, the trench and gates, the eagle-and-serpent
omen, the Lycian assault, Sarpedon’s account of kingly obligation, and Hector’s
stone breach. It does not certify meter or replace an independent omissions/additions
review.

## Findings and actions

| 12.391–471 | The breach must be felt as labor before it becomes catastrophe. | Retain Glaucus’s withdrawal, Epicles’s death, Sarpedon’s assault, the balance simile, Hector’s divinely lightened stone, the hinges, bars, and double gates. |

## Critical notes

- Beauty comes from the collision of architecture and weather: human stakes,
  stones, hinges, and gates meet rivers, snow, dust, and storm. Keep the material
  vocabulary visible beneath the elevated diction.
- The Longfellow-inspired register should make Sarpedon’s speech grave and lucid,
  while Hector’s breach needs shorter, heavier clauses to preserve impact.
- The meter report is only a syllable screen. A named human reader must still
  review stress, proper names, spatial transitions, and the long similes’ breath.
