# Odyssey Book 18 — read-aloud and fidelity review

**Review status:** translator self-review, revision pass; independent reader still required
**Revision date:** 2026-09-03

## Findings

The manuscript preserves the doorway contest as a controlled revelation:
Antinous supplies the wager, Odysseus asks for an oath against interference,
Athena enlarges his body, and he chooses a measured blow so the Achaeans will
not recognize him. Irus’s public humiliation and the warning of King Echetus
remain distinct from the later battle.

Penelope’s appearance follows the contest and is treated as strategy, not mere
ornament. Her staged grief and reminder that suitors should bring gifts rather
than consume another man’s wealth produce the named bride-gifts. The later lamps,
Melantho’s abuse, Eurymachus’s labor challenge, and Amphinomus’s restraint carry
the hall toward violence without importing the bow contest from Book 21.

Penelope’s gift speech and the lamp-lit abuse sequence were expanded into verse
in this pass. The cadence moves from public flattery to strategic extraction,
then narrows into the lamps, Melantho’s threat, and Odysseus’s contained anger;
the revision remains working evidence pending independent review.

## Beauty and cadence

The scene’s comic surface should be allowed to darken gradually. The short,
physical blows of the Irus episode contrast with Penelope’s long, ceremonious
speech; the gift catalogue should feel like wealth extracted from predation.
Odysseus’s inward restraint and public anger carry the moral pressure.

## Open decisions

- Independently verify Irus’s names, Echetus’s threat, the gift inventory, and
  the sequence of lamps and servants against the full Greek.
- Check the cupbearer’s fall and Amphinomus’s intervention for exact agency.
- Commission an independent read-aloud and omissions/additions report.

**Conclusion:** contest, gift, and warning architecture are preserved; not
approved for layout or final publication.
