# Iliad Book 20 — read-aloud and fidelity revision log

**Scope:** Iliad 20.1–503, current working translation  
**Date:** 2026-09-02  
**Status:** editorial working evidence; not a gate pass  
**Reviewer:** AI-assisted draft review; human sign-off required

This pass tests the open divine council, the opposed divine factions, Achilles’
return, the Aeneas encounter, Apollo’s rescue of Hector, and the battle catalogue
through the book’s stated boundary. It does not certify meter or replace an
independent omissions/additions review.

## Findings and actions

| Passage | Finding | Action or decision |
|---|---|---|
| 20.1–116 | The gods’ assembly turns the human war into an openly divided field. | Preserve Themis’s summons, Oceanus’s absence, the divine pairings, Poseidon’s earthquake, and Achilles’ single-minded search for Hector. |
| 20.117–209 | Apollo’s prompting of Aeneas must not erase Aeneas’s own courage or the gods’ calculated restraint. | Retain Apollo’s likeness, Aeneas’s prior defeat and rescue, Hera’s warning, Poseidon’s decision to protect Aeneas, and the gods’ withdrawal to watch. |
| 20.210–406 | The Aeneas duel depends on ancestral speech, shield mechanics, and a rescue that interrupts rather than resolves the contest. | Preserve both genealogies, the spear and shield impacts, Aeneas’s stone, Poseidon’s mist, the removed spear, the lift across the ranks, and the warning about Aeneas’s future. |
| 20.407–470 | Achilles’ return to the ranks broadens from deferred Hector to indiscriminate battlefield force. | Keep Apollo’s warning to Hector, the distinction between divine rescue and mortal fear, and the transition into the battle catalogue. |
| 20.471–503 | The catalogue’s violence must remain legible as a consequence of grief, not a decorative list. | Retain Polydorus, Hector’s defense, Apollo’s cloud, Laogonus and Dardanus, Tros, Mulius, Echeclus, Deucalion, Rigmus, Arēithoön, the fire and threshing similes, and the explicit Book 20 boundary. |

## Critical notes

- Beauty depends on the collision of divine scale and mortal singularity: the gods
  divide the field, but Achilles still seeks one man, and the poem delays that
  meeting through Aeneas and Apollo.
- The Longfellow-inspired register should make the divine council stately and the
  ancestral speeches grave, then let Achilles’ catalogue become increasingly
  physical, rapid, and difficult to contain.
- The meter report is only a syllable screen. A named human reader must still
  review stress, proper names, shield mechanics, divine agency, and the breath of
  the battle catalogue.
