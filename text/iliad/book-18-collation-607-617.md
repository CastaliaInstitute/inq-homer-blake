## Revised translation pass

Upon the outermost rim of the well-wrought shield
he set the great strength of the river Oceanus,
flowing around the world.

When the famous smith had finished the great and mighty shield,
he made for Achilles a corselet brighter than the gleam of fire;
he made a heavy helmet, fitted close to his temples,
beautiful and cunningly wrought, and set upon it a golden crest;
he made greaves of pliant tin, fitted to the ankles.

When the famous smith with crippled feet had completed all the armor,
he lifted it and laid it before Achilles’s mother.
Then, like a hawk, she sprang down from snowy Olympus,
carrying the flashing armor from Hephaestus.

**Book boundary:** the Greek text ends at line 617. The following
reconciliation, gifts, Briseis, nectar, and arming sequence belongs to the
next book and is not included here.
