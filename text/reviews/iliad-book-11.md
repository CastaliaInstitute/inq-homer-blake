## AI-assisted beauty and accuracy review — 2026-09-03

### Strengths

- The book’s governing arc is clear in the verse: bronze catalogue, public
  slaughter, commanders’ wounds, tactical isolation, and finally the intimate
  work of washing and healing. That descent gives the Longfellow-derived
  cadence a genuine emotional architecture rather than a merely elevated tone.
- The strongest images are the reaper, the dry-wood fire, the wounded stag,
  and the river carrying trees to the sea. They remain attached to action and
  do not become detachable ornament.
- Speeches preserve agency and tactical motive: Zeus times Hector’s charge,
  Odysseus argues against flight, Socus boasts before striking, and Nestor’s
  memory becomes the practical charge to Patroclus.

### Accuracy checks and corrections

| Check | Finding | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Named combatants and kinships | Iphidamas/Coon, Socus/Charops, and the paired rescuers remain distinct; the source map covers every named interval. | Retain, then verify against the Greek by an independent reader. |
| Numerical detail | The first verse pass changed Iphidamas’s promised “thousand goats and sheep” to “countless flocks,” losing a source-specific number. | Corrected in `book-11-opening.md` to “a thousand goats / And sheep.” |
| Agency and divine causation | Apollo’s protection of Hector, Zeus’s timed instruction, and Athena’s protection of Odysseus remain explicit. | No change; confirm diction and tense in Greek comparison. |
| Medical sequence | Eurypylus’s arrow extraction, warm washing, bitter root, drying wound, and continuing pain remain in order. | Retain; verify plant/medical terminology in notes review. |

### Beauty risks still open

The repeated “bronze,” “strong,” and “great” diction can flatten hierarchy if
left unchecked; the final line edit should vary syntax without weakening the
epic register. Several battle declarations deliberately use familiar Homeric
formulae, but a human reader should test whether the cadence sounds inherited
from Longfellow’s public blank verse or merely generalized nineteenth-century
elevation. The meter report is a screening aid, not a scansion or beauty pass.
