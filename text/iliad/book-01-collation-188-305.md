## Decisions

- “Visible to him alone” preserves the poem's staging of divine intervention:
  the assembly does not see Athena and cannot share Achilles's perception.
- The oath keeps the staff's material history—cut green from the mountain,
  stripped by bronze, and now carried by Zeus-appointed judges—rather than
  reducing it to an abstract symbol.
- “Three times over” remains a future promise, not a present compensation;
  Athena's intervention stops the blow but does not repair the insult.
- The Hector prophecy retains Achilles's bitter reversal: Agamemnon will need
  the absent fighter while Hector kills Achaeans beside their ships.
- Nestor's counsel distinguishes strength (*karteros*) from royal authority:
  Agamemnon rules more people and therefore holds the greater political rank.
- Achilles's final warning is physical and conditional: he will not fight over
  the girl, but he will defend the other possessions beside his ship.

This is a working collation, not a final approval record. Apparatus consultation
and a second reader's sign-off are still required before the book can advance.
