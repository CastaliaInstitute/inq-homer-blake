# Iliad Book 19 — read-aloud and fidelity revision log

**Scope:** Iliad 19.1–424, current working translation  
**Date:** 2026-09-02  
**Status:** editorial working evidence; not a gate pass  
**Reviewer:** AI-assisted draft review; human sign-off required

This pass tests Thetis’s return with the armor, the public reconciliation,
Agamemnon’s account of Ate, Briseis’s lament, Achilles’ arming, and Xanthus’s
prophecy. It does not certify meter or replace an independent omissions/additions
review.

## Findings and actions

| Passage | Finding | Action or decision |
|---|---|---|
| 19.1–64 | Thetis’s armor delivery and the ambrosial care of Patroclus keep the dead at the center of the public return. | Preserve the armor’s divine origin, Achilles’ sharpened grief, Thetis’s request for assembly, and the nectar and ambrosia placed over Patroclus. |
| 19.65–153 | The wounded leaders’ arrival and Agamemnon’s speech must distinguish explanation from absolution. | Retain the limp of Diomedes and Odysseus, Agamemnon’s oath, the Delusion/Ate history, Hera’s Heracles story, and the explicit admission of responsibility. |
| 19.154–271 | Achilles’s answer moves from anger to action without pretending that reconciliation repairs Patroclus’s death. | Preserve Achilles’ refusal to eat, the harvest simile, the request for gifts before the assembly, and Odysseus’s practical insistence on food and proof. |
| 19.272–357 | The return of gifts and Briseis’s lament shift political compensation into personal mourning. | Keep the inventory, oath sacrifice, Briseis’s memory of Patroclus, and the distinction between the public settlement and the women’s grief. |
| 19.358–424 | The arming sequence culminates in a speaking horse and a prophecy that Achilles cannot evade. | Retain Athena’s nourishment, the snow and beacon similes, the greaves, corselet, shield, helmet, Pelian spear, horse genealogy, Xanthus’s warning, the Erinyes interruption, and the stated Book 19 boundary. |

## Critical notes

- Beauty depends on incomplete repair: gifts can be counted, oaths can be sworn,
  and armor can be fitted, but grief remains the motive power of the scene.
- The Longfellow-inspired register should give the assembly and armor catalogue a
  ceremonial amplitude, then let Briseis’s lament and Xanthus’s prophecy break
  that public surface with intimate knowledge.
- The meter report is only a syllable screen. A named human reader must still
  review stress, proper names, inventory breath, armor terminology, and the shift
  from reconciliation to fatal prophecy.
