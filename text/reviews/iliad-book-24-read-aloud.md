# Iliad Book 24 — read-aloud and fidelity revision log

**Scope:** Iliad 24.1–804, current working translation  
**Date:** 2026-09-03  
**Status:** editorial working evidence; not a gate pass  
**Reviewer:** AI-assisted draft review; human sign-off required

This pass tests the gods’ debate, the ransom journey, Priam’s meeting with
Achilles, the truce, and the three laments. It checks the Longfellow-inspired
cadence against the seven Greek-collation units; it does not certify meter or
replace an independent omissions/additions review.

## Findings and actions

| Passage | Finding | Action or decision |
|---|---|---|
| 24.1–119 | The opening must make Hector’s dishonor last twelve days while Apollo preserves the body and the gods debate whether pity can become action. | Preserve Achilles’s sleepless grief, the threefold dragging, Apollo’s aegis-like protection, Hera’s wedding grievance, and Zeus’s command to Thetis. |
| 24.120–228 | Thetis and Iris carry the same divine instruction into two different human registers: Achilles’s reluctant obedience and Priam’s terror, grief, and resolve. | Retain the single older escort, Hermes’s protection, Hecuba’s warning, the face-to-face divine sign, and Priam’s willingness to die after embracing Hector. |
| 24.229–386 | The ransom inventory is exact and material; the eagle on Priam’s right changes an impossible journey into a sanctioned one. | Preserve the twelve robes and cloaks, twelve rugs and coverlets, twelve tunics, ten talents, tripods, cauldrons, cup, wagon details, Hecuba’s omen prayer, and Zeus’s eagle. |
| 24.387–551 | Hermes’s escort needs to feel both uncanny and practical, ending at the threshold where Priam enters Achilles’s shelter alone. | Keep the young-man likeness, immortal sandals and wand, the ford, the disguised approach, the opened gate, and Hermes’s final self-revelation. |
| 24.552–718 | The central meeting is not reconciliation in the political sense: two enemies mourn separate dead and briefly recognize the fathers behind them. | Preserve the knee-clasp and hand-kiss, the shared weeping for Hector, Peleus, and Patroclus, the washed body, the Niobe example, the meal, and the nine-day truce. |
| 24.719–804 | Cassandra’s cry opens a sequence of three distinct laments, and Priam’s final command restores public ritual without claiming that Troy is safe. | Retain Andromache’s child and captivity fears, Hecuba’s memory of Patroclus’s tomb, Helen’s gratitude for Hector’s kindness, and the twelfth-day protection. |

## Critical craft notes

- The book’s beauty depends on restraint: elevated diction should widen the
  encounter without dissolving the asymmetry between victor and bereaved father.
- The repeated gifts, gates, hands, beds, and coverings form a tactile grammar of
  mercy; keep them concrete rather than summarizing them as “honor.”
- The laments must remain three voices. Andromache looks forward to enslavement,
  Hecuba backward to violence, and Helen inward toward the kindness that made
  life in Troy possible.
- The meter report remains a syllable screen. A named human reader must still
  assess stress, proper names, and the long speeches before the Verse gate passes.
