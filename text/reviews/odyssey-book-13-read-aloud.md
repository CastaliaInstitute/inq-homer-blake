# Odyssey Book 13 — read-aloud and fidelity review

**Review status:** translator self-review; independent reader still required

## Findings

The manuscript preserves the departure’s final gifts and Demodocus, Odysseus’s
impatience for sunset, the uncanny overnight voyage, and the Phorcys landing.
The cave’s two unequal doors, the hidden treasure, the Phaeacian ship turned to
stone, and Alcinous’s twelve-bull response remain part of the narrative rather
than decorative background.

Athena’s encounter is kept as a staged recognition: the disguised shepherd tests
Odysseus, the Cretan tale is a deliberate lie rather than biography, the mist is
lifted so Ithaca can be recognized, and only then does Athena reveal herself and
set the suitor plan in motion. The ending correctly sends Athena toward Lacedaemon
and Telemachus while Odysseus goes to Eumaeus.

## Beauty and cadence

The book’s emotional movement is from possession to estrangement: home is under
his feet but hidden by mist, and recognition arrives through a god’s controlled
revelations. Long descriptive sentences suit the harbor and cave; the shepherd
dialogue should remain quick enough to preserve the mutual testing. The old-beggar
transformation needs a stark, unsentimental cadence.

## Open decisions

- Independently verify the cave furnishings, Phaeacian punishment, and twelve-bull
  offering against the full Greek.
- Check the Cretan lie, suitor chronology, and Telemachus warning for omissions.
- Commission an independent read-aloud and omissions/additions report.

**Conclusion:** principal geography and disguise logic are preserved; not approved
for layout or final publication.
