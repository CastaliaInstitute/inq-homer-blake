# Odyssey Book 1 — read-aloud revision log

**Scope:** Odyssey 1.1–444, current working translation  
**Date:** 2026-09-03  
**Status:** editorial working evidence; not a gate pass  
**Reviewer:** AI-assisted draft review; human sign-off required

This pass checks the opening for narrative poise, audible syntax, tonal
continuity, and fidelity-sensitive compression. It does not certify meter or
Greek accuracy. The Greek-fidelity gate, independent read-aloud, full stress
analysis, and human sign-off remain open.

## Findings and actions

| Passage | Finding | Action or decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1–21 | The invocation must hold the poem's wide geography while turning from wandering to the intimate wound of homecoming. | Revised “Tell me, O Muse … who, having overthrown Troy's sacred citadel, / wandered far” and moved the sea-sorrow phrase out of the awkward “within his heart”; retain “many turnings,” the companions' self-made ruin, and the delayed return. |
| 1.22–50 | Zeus's Aegisthus example risks sounding like a moral aside detached from Odysseus. | Keep the remembered murder, Hermes's warning, Orestes's delayed revenge, and Zeus's final judgment in a compact council cadence; preserve the gods' responsibility debate without adding a modern moral gloss. |
| 1.51–100 | Athena's plan moves from divine permission to Telemachus's public maturation and then into embodied disguise. | Keep the order of Hermes, Ithaca, Pylos, Lacedaemon, sandals, spear, descent, and Mentes's identity; allow the action to accelerate only after the ceremonial equipment is named. |
| 1.101–200 | The hall scene can become inventory: weapons, chairs, water, bread, meat, wine, and the singer. | Preserve the household choreography and the contrast between Telemachus's courtesy and the suitors' consumption; use short visual beats so hospitality reads as moral structure, not furniture description. |
| 1.201–270 | Telemachus's paternity anxiety and the suitors' abuse need emotional clarity without sentimental explanation. | Retain the public taunts, the absent father, the inheritance at risk, and Athena's encouraging replies; mark the change from inward grief to speech through lineation. |
| 1.271–327 | Athena's counsel must sound like a plan that can be enacted, not generalized advice. | Keep the assembly, the ship, the provisions, Pylos, Lacedaemon, and the possibility of report or reputation; preserve the conditional logic of the journey. |
| 1.328–364 | Penelope's interruption turns the hall's song into a contested memory. | Keep Phemius's compelled song, Penelope's grief, Telemachus's rebuke, and the return to household order as separate voices; avoid making Penelope merely decorative. |
| 1.365–444 | Telemachus's closing speech and departure planning need a rising public cadence after the private threshold scene. | Preserve the shame of the suitors, the appeal to the elders, the omen and its interpretation, and the practical preparations; read the final movement toward the ship as a genuine change in agency. |

## Critical notes

- “Many turnings” gives the opening a formal, Longfellow-inspired gravity while
  retaining the Greek's emphasis on movement and resourcefulness; it should be
  tested against any later epithet policy so the phrase does not become a loose
  synonym for every form of cleverness.
- The first draft's phrase “the daughter / of the sea's old strength” obscured
  Atlas. It has been revised to name Atlas and preserve the source's knowledge
  of the sea's depths.
- The opening revision of 2026-09-03 improves the English syntax around Troy,
  the sea-sorrows, and release from toil. It is a beauty-and-clarity pass, not
  a claim that the Greek or meter gates have been cleared.
- The working verse favors blank-verse-like breath groups rather than a rigid
  one-line-per-Greek-line scheme. This is appropriate to the project standard,
  but long council and hall passages need 100% print review in the two-column
  measure.
- The five-page Book 1 architecture proof includes the 1.1–50 excerpt so the
  two-column test exercises more than the invocation alone; this does not
  constitute approval of the full book.
