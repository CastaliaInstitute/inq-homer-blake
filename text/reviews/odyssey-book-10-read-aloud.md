# Odyssey Book 10 — read-aloud and fidelity review

**Review status:** translator self-review; independent reader still required

## Findings

The manuscript preserves the book’s essential reversals: Aeolus binds every
hostile wind and sends the West Wind, Ithaca comes into sight before the crew’s
folly opens the hide, and Aeolus refuses a second rescue because the gods appear
to oppose Odysseus. The Laestrygonian episode correctly leaves one ship alive;
the eleven other ships and their crews are destroyed in the harbor.

The Circe sequence retains the two-day grief, the third-day reconnaissance, the
division under Eurylochus, the human minds inside swine-shapes, Hermes’s moly,
the oath, the year’s delay, and Circe’s instructions for Hades. The review should
guard against making Circe’s hospitality seem uncomplicated: restoration is also
another form of delay.

## Accuracy and cadence

The most important numerical anchors are the nine-day voyage to Ithaca, the
six-day rowing after Aeolus’s refusal, the two-day wait at Aeaea, and the year
spent with Circe. The long retrospective paragraphs should be read against the
short commands at the harbor and Circe’s house; this contrast gives the prose a
Longfellow-like narrative pulse without flattening danger.

## Open decisions

- Independently check Aeolus’s family and island architecture, the harbor details,
  and Circe’s drug, animals, and provisions against the full Greek.
- Verify every measurement and the Hades offering sequence in the final text.
- Commission an independent read-aloud and omissions/additions report.

**Conclusion:** central causal sequence is sound; descriptive omissions and final
line-level meter remain open, so the book is not approved for publication.
