## Revised translation pass

“Or let her hear of it herself when I have set forth,
That she may not, weeping, mar her lovely flesh.”

So he spoke; and the old nurse swore a mighty oath
By all the gods. When she had sworn and ended the oath,
She drew the wine at once into the two-handled jars,
And poured the barley meal into the well-sewn leather bags.

Telemachus went up again and mingled with the suitors.
But the goddess, gray-eyed Athena, conceived another thought.
Taking the likeness of Telemachus, she went throughout the city;
She stood beside each man and spoke, bidding them gather
Beside the swift ship at evening.
Then she sought Noemon, the shining son of Phronius,
And asked him for the swift ship; and he willingly promised it.

The sun went down, and all the streets were shadowed.
Then Noemon drew the swift ship down to the sea,
And laid aboard her all the gear that well-benched ships carry.
He stationed her at the harbor’s outer edge,
And his excellent companions gathered around her;
The goddess herself urged each man onward.

Again the gray-eyed goddess formed another purpose.
She went toward the house of godlike Odysseus,
And there poured sweet sleep upon the suitors;
She made them reel as they drank, and struck the cups
From their hands. They rose to seek their beds throughout the city,
Nor sat much longer at the feast, for sleep fell upon their eyes.

Then gray-eyed Athena called Telemachus forth
From the well-built halls, and, in the form and voice of Mentor,
Spoke to him:
“Telemachus, thy well-greaved companions already sit
At the oars, awaiting thy command to set forth.
Come, let us go; let us not delay long upon the road.”

So she spoke, and Pallas Athena led the way swiftly;
And he followed in the footsteps of the god.
When they came down to the ship and to the sea,
They found their long-haired companions waiting on the shore.
Then the sacred strength of Telemachus addressed them:
“Come, friends; let us carry aboard the provisions;
They are all already gathered in the halls.
My mother knows nothing, nor any of the other maidservants—
One woman alone has heard the word.”

So he spoke and led the way; and they followed after him.
Carrying everything, they laid it in the well-benched ship,
As the dear son of Odysseus had commanded.
Telemachus climbed aboard, and Athena led the way;
She sat down in the stern, and Telemachus sat near her.
They loosed the stern-cables, and themselves went aboard,
Then took their places upon the rowing-benches.

For them gray-eyed Athena sent a moist, fair wind,
The fresh West Wind, sounding over the wine-dark sea.
Telemachus urged his companions, commanding them
To lay hold upon the ship’s gear; and they obeyed his call.
They raised the fir mast within the hollow socket,
Lifted it upright, and bound it with the forestays;
Then drew the white sails aloft with well-twisted oxhide thongs.
The wind filled the middle sail, and the purple wave
Roared loudly round the stern as the ship went forward;
And she ran upon the wave, cleaving her appointed path.

When they had secured the gear aboard the swift black ship,
They set out mixing-bowls crowned with wine,
And poured libations to the gods, immortal and forever-living—
Above all to the gray-eyed daughter of Zeus.
All night the ship cut her course through the sea,
And onward still she pierced it until dawn.

## Decision log

- Complete the oath without adding a false resolution: Eurycleia may tell
  Penelope only after the eleventh and twelfth days have come, preserving the
  source’s concern that grief might damage her body.
- Keep Athena’s practical disguise work distinct from her divine intervention:
  she recruits men in Telemachus’ likeness, then later returns as Mentor.
- Retain Noemon’s willing loan, the sunset launch, the ship’s equipment, and
  the companions gathering at the harbor’s edge.
- Preserve the secrecy count: Penelope and the other maids do not know; one
  woman alone has heard the plan.
- Keep the nautical sequence visible—stern-cables, rowing benches, fir mast,
  forestays, white sails, oxhide thongs, West Wind, and the purple wave—before
  the all-night voyage and libation to Zeus’ daughter.
