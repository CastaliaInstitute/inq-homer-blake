# Translation charter

## Governing source

The English text is new work translated from a documented Greek edition. The
Greek copy text, apparatus consulted, and date of access must be recorded in
`text/sources.md` before translation begins. Existing English translations may
be used for comparison, never as an unacknowledged source.

## Formal model

Henry Wadsworth Longfellow's translation of Dante's *Divine Comedy* is the
principal formal reference. The target is flexible unrhymed iambic pentameter:
a grave, processional line whose cadence can carry long narrative movement.
The model governs tone, meter, and dignity, not wording or content.

## House rules

- Preserve the twenty-four-book structure of both epics.
- Prefer concrete, direct syntax and audible recurrence over explanatory
  paraphrase.
- Retain meaningful epithets, ritual formulas, proper names, and technical
  terms consistently; maintain a glossary when alternatives are unavoidable.
- Let syntax clarify who acts, but do not erase productive Homeric ambiguity.
- Use notes for cultural, textual, and translation difficulty rather than
  burdening the verse with exposition.
- Record every substantial departure from the Greek, disputed reading, and
  later revision in a decision log.

## Review gates

Every book receives separate checks for Greek fidelity, narrative continuity,
meter and cadence, diction, repeated-formula consistency, and editorial notes.
No book is layout-ready until all six checks are signed off.
