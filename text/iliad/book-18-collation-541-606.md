## Revised translation pass

Upon it he set a soft, rich fallow field,
wide and thrice-plowed; many plowmen within it
turned their teams of oxen back and forth.
Whenever they reached the boundary at the end of the field,
a man came forward and gave each a cup
of honey-sweet wine into his hands.
Then they turned again along the furrows,
eager to reach the far boundary of the deep fallow.
Behind them the earth grew dark,
though it was gold; that was the wonder of the work.

Upon it he set a royal estate.
There reapers held sharp sickles in their hands
and cut the standing grain. The sheaves fell thickly
along the furrow; some binders tied them into bundles.
Three binders stood at the work, and behind them boys,
gathering the cut stalks in their arms,
carried them forward without pause.
The king stood among them, silent and glad at heart,
holding his staff upon the furrow.
Apart, beneath an oak, heralds prepared a feast;
they had sacrificed a great ox, while women nearby
scattered much white meal for the reapers’ supper.

Upon it he set a vineyard, beautiful and golden,
heavy with dark clusters of grapes.
The vines stood in rows upon silver poles;
around it ran a dark-blue trench, and around the trench
he laid a fence of tin. One path alone led through it,
by which the bearers walked when they gathered the grapes.
Young women and unwedded youths, innocent in heart,
carried the honey-sweet fruit in woven baskets.
Among them a boy with a clear lyre played sweetly
and sang the lovely song of Linos in a delicate voice;
the others followed, striking the ground together,
leaping with their feet, their song and cry in time.

Upon it he set a herd of straight-horned cattle.
The cows were fashioned of gold and tin;
with lowing they hurried from the dung-yard
to the pasture beside the resounding river,
beside the trembling reeds.
Four golden herdsmen went in line with the cattle,
and nine white-footed dogs followed them.
Two terrible lions among the first cattle
held a bellowing, deep-roaring bull.
The bull they dragged away, lowing long;
dogs and young men pursued them.
The lions tore open the hide of the great bull,
gulped down his entrails and dark blood,
while the herdsmen sent the swift dogs against them.
But the dogs held back from biting the lions;
they stood very near and barked, then drew away,
afraid to come within their jaws.

Upon it the famous smith made a pasture
in a lovely valley, for great flocks of white sheep,
with covered folds, huts, and roofed pens.

Upon it the famous smith wrought a dancing floor,
like the one Daedalus once made in broad Knossos
for Ariadne of the beautiful hair.
There young men and maidens whose wealth is won by labor
danced, holding one another’s wrists.
The maidens wore fine linen; the young men wore tunics
carefully woven, shining softly with oil.
The maidens wore beautiful crowns;
the young men had golden knives hanging from silver baldrics.
Now they ran with practiced feet, easily,
as when a potter sitting at his work tries a wheel,
holding it in his palms to see if it will run;
and now they ran in ordered rows toward one another.
A great crowd stood around the lovely dance,
delighting in it; among them a divine singer
played upon his lyre, while two tumblers
whirled through the midst, leading the song and dance.

**Continuation:** line 607 begins the Oceanus border and completes the
shield’s encircling design; this unit ends after the two tumblers’ movement
through the dance.
