## Decisions

- The council before the general assembly is restored. Agamemnon first reports
  the Dream to the elders, and Nestor's acceptance precedes the public test.
- The scepter genealogy is retained as political history, ending with
  Agamemnon's authority over islands and Argos rather than treating the staff
  as a generic sign of office.
- Agamemnon's speech keeps its self-contradictory surface: he calls Zeus's
  message a delusion, lists the cost of the war, and then orders the test that
  the soldiers hear as a real command to flee.
- The number comparison, the cupbearer thought experiment, the nine-year delay,
  rotting ships, and waiting families remain in the speech as pressure on the
  assembly's exhaustion.
- Athena's warning preserves Helen as the disputed glory and the Achaean dead
  as the moral cost of abandonment.
- Odysseus uses persuasion with kings and force with common men. The translation
  retains this unequal civic order rather than smoothing it into consensus.

This is a working collation, not a final approval record. Apparatus consultation
and a second reader's sign-off are still required before the book can advance.
