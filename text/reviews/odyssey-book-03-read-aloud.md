# Odyssey Book 3 — read-aloud revision log

**Scope:** Odyssey 3.1–497, current working translation  
**Date:** 2026-09-03  
**Status:** editorial working evidence; not a gate pass  
**Reviewer:** AI-assisted draft review; human sign-off required

This pass checks the Pylian sacrifice, Nestor's fragmented return account,
Helen's story and drug, Proteus's report, and the departure sequence for
ceremonial cadence and fidelity-sensitive compression. It does not certify
meter or Greek accuracy. Independent source comparison, full stress analysis,
and human sign-off remain open.

## Findings and actions

| Passage | Finding | Action or decision |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1–38 | The opening sacrifice establishes Pylos as a public, sea-facing community before Telemachus is recognized. | Preserve the nine companies, five hundred men per company, and nine bulls per company; keep Peisistratus's welcome and the libation before Nestor's questions. |
| 3.39–101 | Telemachus's request must sound newly courageous but still dependent on the ritual authority of Nestor's knees. | Retain the deaths of the other chiefs, the uncertainty over Odysseus, and the direct appeal; avoid making his speech sound like a modern interview. |
| 3.102–174 | Nestor's return account is deliberately partial and self-locating, not a complete narrative of the Achaean homecomings. | Keep the quarrel of the Atreids, the separate departures, the Lesbos route, the Zeus omen, and the named return paths; mark “I heard” as hearsay where the Greek does. |
| 3.175–229 | Nestor's list of the dead and absent must carry grief without closing the search for Odysseus. | Preserve Achilles, Patroclus, Ajax, Agamemnon, and Menelaus as distinct memories; do not turn the list into an authoritative final register. |
| 3.230–286 | Athena's intervention changes a guest's itinerary into a guided journey. | Keep Mentor's form, Peisistratus's youthful role, the gifts, and the second prayer as separate ritual and narrative beats. |
| 3.287–355 | The move from sea travel to Menelaus's marriage feast risks becoming a travel summary. | Preserve the night voyage, Laconian arrival, household welcome, and the splendor that prompts Telemachus's comparison with Zeus. |
| 3.356–418 | Helen's recognition, drug, and Troy story combine consolation with self-fashioning. | Keep the drug's extreme promise, Helen's knowledge of the beggar, and the tears' snow image; do not resolve the ethical ambiguity through commentary in the verse. |
| 3.419–497 | Proteus's report and the gifts lead into a prophetic departure rather than a simple conclusion. | Retain Calypso's island, the silver-and-gold gifts, the robe, eagle omen, Pherae stop, and renewed road; read the ending as delayed knowledge becoming motion. |

## Critical notes

- The book's formal strength is its hospitality architecture: sacrifice, shared
  food, washing, story, gift, sleep, and renewed travel. The elevated diction
  should serve that sequence rather than make every welcome sound identical.
- Proper names and return routes need concordance review across both epics.
  Nestor's account is especially vulnerable to accidental harmonization with
  later books.
- The corrected opening number now preserves the source's scale: nine groups
  of five hundred, each with nine bulls. It should be checked against the Greek
  collation once more by a second reader before any gate is considered.
