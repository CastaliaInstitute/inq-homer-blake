# Iliad Book 2 — read-aloud revision log

**Scope:** Iliad 2.1–877, current working translation  
**Date:** 2026-09-03  
**Status:** editorial working evidence; not a gate pass  
**Reviewer:** AI-assisted draft review; human sign-off required

This pass checks the book's deceptive-dream opening, assembly movement,
Thersites episode, battle muster, and long catalogue for audible architecture
and fidelity-sensitive compression. It does not certify meter or Greek
accuracy. Independent source comparison, full stress analysis, and human
sign-off remain open.

## Findings and actions

| Passage | Finding | Action or decision |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1–47 | The false dream must sound authoritative to Agamemnon while remaining legible as Zeus's deception to the reader. | Preserve the repeated sleep/waking frame, Nestor's likeness, the imperative to arm, and the closing irony that the promise will not be fulfilled; do not make the Dream's voice theatrically sinister. |
| 2.48–83 | Agamemnon's waking report compresses the dream and risks losing the difference between what he saw and what Zeus actually planned. | Keep the council setting, the “test” of the army, and the order in which the dream, flight proposal, and Nestor's assent occur; retain the narrative irony without explanatory notes inside the verse. |
| 2.84–154 | The bee simile and the gathering assembly need a broad, controlled musical expansion before the scepter speech. | Let the bees, Rumor, earth, heralds, and settling noise arrive as separate waves; retain the nine heralds and the scepter's genealogy as structural preparation. |
| 2.155–242 | Agamemnon's deceptive test becomes a genuine flight when the people rush toward the ships. | Preserve the ship-rot, absent families, numerical comparison, and Icarian/South-Wind/West-Wind movement; do not flatten the speech into a simple military pep talk. |
| 2.243–324 | Hera and Athena redirect the assembly, while Odysseus must distinguish kings from common soldiers in his use of the scepter. | Maintain the contrast between courteous persuasion and physical rebuke; keep the scepter as a changing instrument of civic order rather than a generic royal prop. |
| 2.325–393 | Thersites is intentionally ugly, disorderly, and rhetorically dangerous; the scene can become either comic caricature or modern political commentary. | Preserve the bodily description, the crooked speech, Achilles' exceptional treatment, and Odysseus's chastisement; review the register for cruelty without adding a moral verdict. |
| 2.394–493 | The assembly's renewed consent and sacrifice must shift from ridicule to disciplined muster. | Keep the Aulis memory, the nine-year duration, Calchas's serpent omen, and Nestor's sorting by tribes and clans as distinct narrative hinges. |
| 2.494–650 | The catalogue's transition from council to ranks risks making genealogy and logistics inaudible. | Preserve named commanders, homeland, ship counts, and the distinction between contingent identity and general praise; use consistent epithet and place-name forms across the glossary. |
| 2.651–877 | The Trojan muster is a long visual procession whose value lies in geographic and political plurality, not mere enumeration. | Read in measured blocks separated by command changes and geographic turns; retain Iris's Polites disguise, Batieia/Myrine, the allied languages, and the final Lycian pair without compressing them into a summary. |

## Critical notes

- The book's strongest Longfellow-inspired feature is its deliberate change of
  scale: intimate sleep, public assembly, comic violence, omen, and catalogue.
  The lineation should protect those changes rather than make every passage use
  the same elevated speed.
- Catalogue diction must be checked against a cross-book proper-name and place
  concordance before layout. Repeated epithets should be stable unless a
  deliberate variation is recorded.
- The machine meter report is especially weak evidence for catalogue passages,
  where Greek names and geographic clusters naturally produce intentional
  outliers. Those outliers require human classification, not automatic
  smoothing.
