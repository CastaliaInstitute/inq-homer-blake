## Revised translation pass

Prudent Telemachus answered him:
“Antinous, there is no way to send from the house against her will
The mother who bore and raised me, while my father is elsewhere—
Whether he lives or has died. And I should pay harshly to Icarius
If I myself sent his daughter away unwillingly.
From my father I should suffer punishment, and from another god as well,
Since my mother, departing, would call the hateful Erinyes
Against me from the house; and the reproach of men would be mine.
Therefore I will never speak that word.

“But if your own hearts are indignant at your acts,
Go forth from my halls, and provide other feasts for yourselves,
Eating your own possessions and taking turns among your houses.
But if you think this better and more profitable—
That one man’s livelihood should perish without repayment—
Consume it. I shall call upon the gods who live forever,
If Zeus may ever grant that such deeds be paid back;
Then, without repayment, you shall perish within these halls.”

So he spoke; and wide-seeing Zeus sent two eagles down
From the mountain’s high crest. For a while they flew together,
Close beside each other, stretching their wings upon the wind.
But when they reached the middle of the many-voiced assembly,
They wheeled and beat their thick wings, and looked down
Upon the heads of all, foreseeing destruction.
With their talons they tore their cheeks and necks on either side,
Then swept away to the right, through the houses and the city.
Those who saw the birds were amazed, and each within his heart
Wondered what the sign would bring to pass.

Then the aged hero Halitherses, son of Mastor, rose among them.
He alone surpassed all men of his age in knowing birds
And in speaking what was fitting. With wise intent he addressed them:

“Hear me now, Ithacans, while I say what I must say.
I speak this especially to the suitors: a great trouble
Is rolling toward them. Odysseus will not remain long
Away from his friends; already, perhaps, he is near,
Planting death and destruction here for them all.
And many others who dwell in clear-seen Ithaca shall suffer,
Unless we consider well, long before it comes to pass,
How we may bring this to an end. Let the suitors themselves
Cease; for it is better for them to cease at once.

“I am not unskilled in prophecy; I know well what I speak.
For I say that everything has been fulfilled for that man
As I told him when the Argives went up into Ilium,
And with them went Odysseus, rich in counsel.
I said that he would suffer many evils, lose all his companions,
And return unknown to all in the twentieth year.
All these things are now being fulfilled.”

## Decision log

- Preserve Telemachus’ refusal as layered obligation: mother, father, Icarius,
  Erinyes, and the reproach of men, rather than a claim of simple legal power.
- Keep his repeated alternative to the suitors—leave and eat their own goods—
  and the final appeal to Zeus for repayment without blood-price.
- Retain Zeus’s two eagles, their close flight, wheeling over the assembly,
  tearing cheeks and necks, and departure to the right through the city.
- Preserve Halitherses’ authority as specific expertise in birds and fitting
  speech, not as an omniscient prophetic narrator.
- Keep the prediction’s exact structure: Odysseus near, death for the suitors,
  danger for Ithaca, and the earlier twentieth-year forecast now fulfilled.
