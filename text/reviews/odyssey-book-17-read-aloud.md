# Odyssey Book 17 — read-aloud and fidelity review

**Review status:** translator self-review; independent reader still required

## Findings

The manuscript preserves the book’s three crossings: Telemachus returns openly,
Eumaeus escorts the beggar through the countryside, and Odysseus enters the hall
before any human recognition. Penelope’s report, Theoclymenus’s prophecy, and
the suitors’ response remain separate from the earlier shipboard hawk omen.

Argos’s recognition is kept as the emotional center of the arrival: the neglected
hound knows Odysseus after twenty years, cannot reach him, and dies only after
seeing his master. Melanthius’s abuse at the spring and Antinous’s footstool blow
remain distinct acts of household violence, with Odysseus choosing restraint for
the larger plan.

## Beauty and cadence

The road should descend from public welcome into insult, then pause at Argos’s
stillness before the hall resumes its noise. The dog’s recognition benefits from
plain diction and a short breath; the suitors’ speeches should feel expansive and
self-satisfied. Penelope’s postponed interview creates a deliberately suspended
close, suitable to Longfellow-like narrative anticipation.

## Open decisions

- Independently verify Argos’s condition, the spring geography, and the exact
  sequence of the two omens and Penelope’s messages.
- Check the Cretan report’s relationship to Book 14 and the timing of the sunset
  interview.
- Commission an independent read-aloud and omissions/additions report.

**Conclusion:** arrival and recognition architecture are preserved; not approved
for layout or final publication.
