# Iliad Book 22 — read-aloud and fidelity revision log

**Scope:** Iliad 22.1–515, current working translation  
**Date:** 2026-09-03  
**Status:** editorial working evidence; not a gate pass  
**Reviewer:** AI-assisted draft review; human sign-off required

This pass tests Hector’s last stand, the chase around Troy, the divinely forced
duel, Achilles’ treatment of Hector’s body, and the first laments. It checks the
Longfellow-inspired cadence against the six Greek-collation units; it does not
certify meter or replace an independent omissions/additions review.

## Findings and actions

| Passage | Finding | Action or decision |
|---|---|---|
| 22.1–90 | The opening’s autumn Dog Star turns Achilles into an omen seen from Priam’s tower; Priam and Hecuba’s appeals make Hector’s decision a family scene before it becomes a duel. | Preserve the star’s fever-bearing image, the remembered deaths of Lycaon and Polydorus, the dogs at Priam’s door, and Hecuba’s physical breast-offering. |
| 22.91–166 | Hector’s inward debate must retain both shame and tactical intelligence: he knows the city’s reproach, imagines a negotiated return of Helen’s goods, then rejects surrender. | Keep Polydamas’s counsel, the proposed division of treasure, the oak-and-rock comparison, the springs and washing-places, and the three circuits of Troy. |
| 22.167–247 | Zeus’s pity is checked by fate, while Athena’s deception converts the impossible chase into a mortal encounter. | Preserve the scales of death, Apollo’s abandonment, Achilles’s pause, Athena’s likeness and voice of Deiphobus, and Hector’s recognition only after the brother fails to appear. |
| 22.248–366 | The refusal of oaths is deliberately brutal; the spear mechanics and exposed neck make the killing precise rather than vague. | Retain Hector’s request for reciprocal burial, Athena’s unseen recovery of Achilles’s spear, the empty hand of Deiphobus, the throat wound that leaves Hector speech, and the prophecy of Paris and Apollo. |
| 22.367–436 | Achilles’ victory becomes desecration, and the city’s grief expands from Hector’s body to Priam’s threatened old age. | Preserve the armor-stripping, tendon binding, head-dragging, Priam’s dung-rolling appeal, and the transition into Hecuba’s lament. |
| 22.437–515 | Andromache’s delayed knowledge and the abandoned bath give the close its domestic pathos; her ornaments and Eetion memory must remain concrete. | Retain the weaving, tripod and bath, the cry from the tower, Andromache’s collapse, and Aphrodite’s bridal gifts. The unit remains source-collated through its stated boundary. |

## Critical craft notes

- The chase should sound almost inexorable, then suddenly intimate: long running
  sentences give way to short terms, refusals, and the exposed throat.
- Longfellow-like elevation is most effective in Priam’s lament and the star and
  springs similes; Achilles’s threats should remain hard and plain, without a
  decorative gloss that blunts their moral violence.
- The repetitions at the source-unit boundaries are checked as transitions, not
  silently treated as new narrative events; the full Greek intervals remain
  linked in the packet for independent comparison.
- The meter report is a syllable screen only. Names, compounds, and the rapid
  dialogue require a named human read-aloud editor before the Verse gate passes.
