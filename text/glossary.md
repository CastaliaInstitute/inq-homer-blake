# Shared Homeric glossary

This is a living consistency register, not a list of mandatory one-to-one
equivalents. A choice may change when syntax, scene, or scholarship requires
it, but the decision must be logged and applied consistently where the same
formula or concept recurs.

| Greek | Working English | Scope and rationale |
|---|---|---|
| *mēnis* (μῆνις) | anger / wrath | Divine-scale anger in the *Iliad* opening; do not casually reduce it to irritation. |
| *polytropos* (πολύτροπος) | man of many turnings | *Odyssey* 1.1; preserves resourcefulness and the poem's opening ambiguity. |
| *kleos* (κλέος) | fame / renown | Choose by context; includes what is heard and remembered after death. |
| *timē* (τιμή) | honor / due | Social standing and the material recognition attached to it. |
| *xenos* (ξεῖνος) | guest / host / stranger | Translate the active social relation, not merely “foreigner.” |
| *nostos* (νόστος) | return / homecoming | A journey home with moral and narrative weight. |
| *aēdōn* (ἀοιδός) | singer | Prefer “singer” where the performance role matters; avoid importing modern “bard” without reason. |
| *athanatos* (ἀθάνατος) | deathless | A recurring divine descriptor; retain the contrast with mortal life. |

## Consistency checks

- Preserve a character's primary epithet unless the syntax or scene makes a
  deliberate variation necessary.
- Keep names and transliteration consistent within each volume; record any
  divergence between volumes here before layout.
- Do not use the glossary to conceal uncertainty. Mark unresolved terms and
  carry them into the relevant book decision log.
