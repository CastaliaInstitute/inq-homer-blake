## Translation

Pallas Athena went to broad Lacedaemon,
To urge the glorious son of Odysseus home.
She found Telemachus and Nestor's son asleep
Before the house of Menelaus. Peisistratus
Lay deep in sleep; but care for his father woke
Telemachus through the ambrosial night.

“Telemachus,” she said, “it is no longer fitting
To wander far from home, while arrogant men
Divide your wealth. They will consume it all.
Urge Menelaus to send you home, before
You find your blameless mother pressed by father,
Brothers, and Eurymachus, who wins the suitors' eyes
With gifts and enlarges the bride-price. Take care
Lest treasure leave your house against your will.
You know how a woman's heart, when she marries again,
Works to increase the house of him she weds,
Forgetting former children and a former husband dead.

“The suitors' best men lie in wait for you
In the strait between Ithaca and rugged Samos,
Desiring to kill you before you reach your land.
Keep your good ship far from the islands; sail by night.
An immortal guardian will send a wind behind you.
When first you reach Ithaca, send ship and friends
Toward the city; but go first to Eumaeus,
Who keeps your pigs and knows your house's grief.
Spend there the night; send him at dawn to tell
Prudent Penelope that you are safe and home from Pylos.”

Athena departed. Telemachus woke Peisistratus:
“Rise, yoke the single-hoofed horses; let us go.”
Peisistratus counseled waiting for dawn, and Menelaus
Came from Helen's chamber. Telemachus asked to leave;
The king agreed, saying a host should send
The willing stranger gladly, not hold him back.
He brought the guest gifts: a silver mixing-bowl,
Given him by Phaedimus of Sidon, and Helen brought
A many-colored robe she had made herself,
“To wear when your bride comes, until then let it lie
Beside your mother.” They ate, and drove away.

Menelaus stood before the horses with sweet wine:
“Greet Nestor. He was gentle to me as a father
While we fought at Troy.” Telemachus answered:
“If I return and find Odysseus in my house,
I will tell him of all kindness shown by you.”
Then an eagle on the right carried a great white goose
Away from the yard. Helen interpreted the sign:
“As the eagle seized the goose from the place where it was bred,
So Odysseus, after suffering and wandering long,
Will come home and punish every suitor—perhaps
He is already there, preparing evil for them all.”
Telemachus prayed that Zeus might fulfill her word,
And drove through the day to Pherae, where Diocles
Gave them a night's hospitality. At dawn they reached Pylos.

Telemachus asked Peisistratus not to drive him
Past the ship: Nestor might hold him from his purpose,
But he must reach the sea quickly. The prince agreed,
Turned the horses to the shore, and put aboard
The clothing and gold that Menelaus had given.
Telemachus called the companions to embark,
Prayed and sacrificed to Athena, and a stranger came—
Theoclymenus, a seer of Melampus' line,
Fleeing Argos after killing one of his own people.
“Take me aboard,” he begged; “they pursue me.”
Telemachus received him, promising to share
Whatever the gods supplied. They raised the mast,
And Athena sent a fierce favorable wind.

At sea Theoclymenus sat beside Telemachus,
While the ship passed Cruni, Chalcis, Phea, and Elis.
In Eumaeus' hut the disguised Odysseus tested the swineherd:
“Would you still keep me here, or send me to the city?
There I might beg before the suitors, and serve them well:
I know how to kindle fire, split wood, carve and roast meat,
And pour the wine that lesser men pour for their betters.”
Eumaeus groaned: “What thought is this? Their violence
Reaches the heavens. Stay here; when Telemachus comes,
He will clothe you and send you where your heart commands.”

Odysseus asked about Laertes and his mother. Eumaeus said:
“Laertes lives, but prays for death within his house.
He mourns his son and the wise wife whose death
Brought him down in painful age. While she lived,
She raised me with Ctimene, and honored me nearly as her own.
When Ctimene married in Same, her last gift was a cloak,
Tunic, and sandals; she sent me to the fields.
Now I lack these things, but the gods make my labor prosper.

“I was born on Syria, an island above Ortygia,
Rich in cattle, sheep, wine, and grain; its two cities
My father Ctesius ruled. Phoenician traders came there.
A beautiful Phoenician woman in our house was enticed
By one of them. She told her story: Taphian pirates
Had stolen her from Sidon and sold her to my father.
The sailor swore to carry her home; she warned him
To keep silent, then promised to bring her child aboard
For a great price. When the ship was full, she hid
Three cups beneath her breast and led me out.

“At the harbor the ship sailed with a fair wind.
For six days we traveled. On the seventh Artemis
Struck the woman, and they cast her into the sea.
Wind and water brought me to Ithaca, where Laertes
Bought me with his possessions. Thus I first saw this land.”

Telemachus' companions now reached the shore. They lowered
The mast, drew the ship into harbor, and ate breakfast.
Telemachus said: “Take the black ship to the city;
I will inspect the farms and herds, and at evening go down.
At dawn I will give you meat and sweet wine.”
Theoclymenus asked whose house should receive him.
Telemachus named Eurymachus, though he feared the suitors,
And said that Zeus alone knew whether their evil day
Would come before the marriage. A hawk on the right,
Apollo's messenger, flew between the ship and shore,
Clutching a dove and scattering feathers. Theoclymenus
Declared the omen: no family in Ithaca was more royal;
Telemachus' line would remain strong.

Telemachus entrusted the seer to Peiraeus,
Who would honor him until his return. Then he boarded,
Took spear and sandals, and the companions rowed
Toward the city. He went ahead on foot and reached
The yard where the countless swine were kept,
Where Eumaeus, faithful and gentle to his masters,
Lived through the year.

## Decision log

- The production-facing opening preserves Athena's practical warning, the
  separate eagle and hawk omens, Menelaus's and Helen's distinct gifts, and
  Theoclymenus's arrival as a suppliant seer.
- Eumaeus's full Syria biography remains a story told in the hut, including
  the Phoenician woman, three hidden cups, Artemis's arrow, and Laertes' sale.
- Book 15 ends with Telemachus reaching Eumaeus's yard. The hut reunion and
  recognition belong to Book 16 and are not imported here.
- This draft requires independent Greek-fidelity review, literary review,
  meter review, notes review, and production sign-off before promotion beyond
  `draft`.
