# Odyssey Book 5 — read-aloud revision log

**Scope:** Odyssey 5.1–493, current working translation  
**Date:** 2026-09-03  
**Status:** editorial working evidence; not a gate pass  
**Reviewer:** AI-assisted draft review; human sign-off required

This pass checks the divine council, Calypso's release, raft construction,
Poseidon's storm, Ino's veil, the river supplication, and Odysseus's landing
for narrative pressure, physical clarity, and fidelity-sensitive compression.
It does not certify meter or Greek accuracy. Independent source comparison, full
stress analysis, and human sign-off remain open.

## Findings and actions

| Passage | Finding | Action or decision |
|---|---|---|
| 5.1–42 | The gods' decision must make Odysseus's release feel inevitable without making his survival effortless. | Preserve Athena's advocacy, Zeus's command, Hermes's wand and sandals, Ogygia, and Calypso's hospitality before the order is delivered. |
| 5.43–101 | Calypso's complaint and the raft's construction hold care and captivity in tension. | Keep the comparison with gods who love mortals, the oath, twenty trees, tools, mast, deck, bulwarks, provisions, and four-day/fifth-day work sequence. |
| 5.102–147 | Poseidon's storm must reverse the calm voyage while preserving Odysseus's self-knowledge and fear of an anonymous death. | Retain the four winds, broken sail and mast, wreckage, the Troy comparison, and the leather strap; do not turn the storm into generalized spectacle. |
| 5.148–196 | Ino's intervention is a second uncertain gift after Calypso's release. | Preserve Ino's mortal past, immortal veil, instructions for casting it away, and Odysseus's suspicion that a god may be setting a snare. |
| 5.197–261 | Athena's calming of the winds and the sea's continuing force must remain distinct. | Keep the two days and two nights, third-day breakers, reefs, cliffs, rock grip, torn skin, and search for a river mouth as separate physical thresholds. |
| 5.262–350 | The river prayer changes the hero from swimmer to suppliant, and the river's response must not be mistaken for ordinary geography. | Retain the unnamed river's divine possibility, stilling current, salt vomiting, Ino's returned veil, and Athena's withdrawal of the sea's force. |
| 5.351–423 | The final plant image should provide concealment without sentimental rest. | Preserve the two olives from one root, the leaf bed, the depth of the covering, and Athena's sleep as exhausted survival. |
| 5.424–493 | The source-range boundary and end of Book 5 should close on vulnerability before Nausicaa's intervention. | Keep the final uncertainty and avoid adding a triumphal arrival; verify the range once more against the pinned source before layout. |

## Critical notes

- The book's strongest formal movement is from divine decree to manual labor to
  elemental ordeal to suppliant stillness. The lineation should preserve that
  descent in scale.
- The translation generally maintains Longfellow-inspired ceremonial clarity,
  but the storm passage contains many short physical actions. Human reading
  should test whether they form a rising sequence or a list.
- Divine agency must remain layered: Zeus orders, Hermes carries the command,
  Calypso equips, Poseidon destroys, Ino rescues, Athena moderates, and the
  river receives. The verse should not collapse these into “the gods.”
- The compact opening now names the Pleiades, late-sinking Boötes, and the Bear,
  and states that Odysseus keeps the Bear on his left. This restores the
  voyage’s navigational agency from the Greek collation; meter and proper-name
  treatment remain open for the independent reader.
