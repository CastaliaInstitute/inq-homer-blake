## Revised translation pass

“Now listen closely and heed what I shall say.
Tomorrow call the Achaean heroes to assembly;
Declare my word to all, and let the gods bear witness.
Bid the suitors scatter each to his own house;
And if thy mother’s heart is moved to marry,
Let her return to the great house of her father.
There they shall make the marriage feast and prepare
The abundant bride-price that should follow a beloved child.

“For thee I give this counsel, if thou wilt obey:
Fit out a ship with twenty oarsmen, the best thou hast,
And go to inquire concerning thy father, long absent—
Whether some mortal may tell thee, or whether a voice
From Zeus may reach thee, the voice that carries fame among men.
First go to Pylos, and ask the noble Nestor;
Then from there to Sparta, to fair-haired Menelaus,
Who was the last of the bronze-clad Achaeans to return.
If thou shouldst hear that thy father lives and is coming home,
Then, though sorely pressed, endure for one more year.
But if thou hearest that he is dead and is no longer among men,
Return to thy dear native land, raise his mound,
And give him the many funeral gifts that are his due;
Then give thy mother to another husband.

“When thou hast finished and performed these things,
Consider deeply in thy mind and heart how thou mayest kill
The suitors within thy halls, whether by cunning or openly.
Thou must no longer carry a child’s thoughts, since thou art
No longer of such an age. Hast thou not heard what fame
The noble Orestes won among all mankind,
When he killed Aegisthus, the plotting murderer,
Who had killed his famous father? Thou too, my friend—
For I see thee beautiful and tall—be brave, so that men yet unborn
May speak well of thee.”

“But I must go down now to my swift ship and my companions,
Who are surely impatient as they wait for me.
Let thy care be thine own, and remember all my words.”

Prudent Telemachus answered her:
“Stranger, thou hast spoken with a friendly heart, like a father
To his child; I shall never forget thy words. But come, wait here,
Though thou art eager for the road, until thou hast bathed,
Refreshed thy heart, and gone to thy ship rejoicing,
Bearing from me a gift of honor, beautiful and precious,
Such as dear friends give to friends, to keep as a treasure.”

Then gray-eyed Athena answered him:
“Do not hold me back any longer, eager as I am for the road.
Whatever gift thy heart commands thee to give me,
Give it to me when I return, that I may carry it home;
Choose something beautiful, and it shall be worthy of return.”

So she spoke, and gray-eyed Athena departed,
And flew away like a bird into the air. She placed
Strength and courage in his heart, and made him remember
His father more strongly than before. He understood within himself,
And wonder seized his spirit; for he thought that she was a god.
At once, godlike in his bearing, he went among the suitors.

Among them the famous singer sang, and they sat in silence,
Listening. He sang of the grievous return of the Achaeans,
The return which Pallas Athena had ordained.

## Decision log

- Preserve Athena’s movement from prophecy to actionable civic instruction:
  assembly, suitors’ dispersal, maternal remarriage, ship, inquiry, and burial.
- Retain the exact journey sequence through Pylos and Sparta, with Menelaus as
  the last bronze-clad Achaean to return, and the one-year endurance condition.
- Keep the alternatives for the suitors—cunning or open violence—without
  collapsing Athena’s counsel into a simple heroic command.
- Preserve the Orestes parallel as public fame among all mankind, tied to the
  murder of Aegisthus and the recovery of paternal honor.
- Retain Telemachus’ hospitality even after receiving urgent counsel, and
  Athena’s refusal of delay as she promises a return-gift instead.
- End with Athena’s bird-like departure, the inward placement of strength and
  courage, and the singer’s grievous Achaean return-song at line 327.
