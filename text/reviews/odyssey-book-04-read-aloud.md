# Odyssey Book 4 — read-aloud revision log

**Scope:** Odyssey 4.1–847, current working translation  
**Date:** 2026-09-03  
**Status:** editorial working evidence; not a gate pass  
**Reviewer:** AI-assisted draft review; human sign-off required

This pass checks the Spartan welcome, recognition of Telemachus, Menelaus's
Egyptian narrative, Helen's Trojan memories, the suitors' ambush, and
Penelope's dream for narrative pacing, ceremonial register, and fidelity-sensitive
compression. It does not certify meter or Greek accuracy. Independent source
comparison, full stress analysis, and human sign-off remain open.

## Findings and actions

| Passage | Finding | Action or decision |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1–67 | The wedding feast and threshold welcome must establish abundance without erasing the strangers' vulnerability. | Preserve the double wedding, Eteoneus's question, Menelaus's guest-friendship rebuke, the horses, baths, oil, garments, and honored food. |
| 4.68–167 | Recognition is a shared act among Helen, Menelaus, and Peisistratus, not a single revelation. | Keep Telemachus's private wonder, Menelaus's grief, Helen's entrance with attendants, and the purple-cloak gesture; avoid modern interior explanation. |
| 4.168–305 | Menelaus's wealth catalogue must turn toward loss, not function as decorative travel writing. | Retain the eight-year wandering, named regions, horned lambs, murdered brother, and distinction between general mourning and grief for Odysseus. |

| 4.431–624 | Helen's drug and Trojan stories carry consolation, guilt, and self-fashioning simultaneously. | Keep the extreme promise of the drug, the wooden horse, Anticlus's restraint, and Odysseus's beggar disguise without resolving Helen's ethical ambiguity. |

| 4.768–847 | The ambush, Penelope's prayer, and Iphthime dream cross public threat with private fear. | Keep Medon's report, the upper chamber, the sacrificial memory, the dream's narrow reassurance, and Penelope's unresolved question about Odysseus. ## Critical notes

- The book's strongest formal opposition is between houses that possess abundance
  and families that possess only memory. The lineation should allow gifts,
  stories, and grief to remain materially distinct.
- The Proteus sequence is a fidelity-sensitive catalogue: omitted forms change
  the texture of the ordeal even when the plot remains understandable. The
  restored boar must be checked against the locked Greek by a second reader.
- Proper names, place names, kinship, and gift descriptions require concordance
  review across Odyssey Books 3–4 before layout.
- The machine meter report cannot determine whether Menelaus's long speeches
  gather ceremonial force or become prose. Human read-aloud review remains
  necessary at the comic-size two-column measure.
