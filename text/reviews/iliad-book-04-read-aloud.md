# Iliad Book 4 — read-aloud revision log

**Scope:** Iliad 4.1–544, current working translation
**Date:** 2026-09-03
**Status:** editorial working evidence; not a gate pass  
**Reviewer:** AI-assisted draft review; human sign-off required

This pass checks the divine council, the broken truce, Pandarus's arrow,
Menelaus's wound, and Agamemnon's movement through the ranks for cadence,
narrative causality, and fidelity-sensitive compression. It does not certify
meter or Greek accuracy. Independent source comparison, full stress analysis,
and human sign-off remain open.

## Critical notes

- The divine opening now has a clear vocal architecture: Zeus provokes, Athena
  withholds, Hera answers, and Zeus yields. This prevents the council from
  sounding like undifferentiated divine chatter.
- The book's Longfellow-inspired strength is its public rhetoric: vows become
  battle, grief becomes command, and genealogy becomes civic pressure. The
  elevated diction should retain those transformations without ornamental
  inflation.
- The arrow and healer passages are especially vulnerable to compression. A
  second reader should verify that no layer of the wound or medical action has
  disappeared during later cadence work.
