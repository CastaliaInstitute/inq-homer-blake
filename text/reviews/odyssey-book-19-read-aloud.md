# Odyssey Book 19 — read-aloud and fidelity review

**Review status:** translator self-review; independent reader still required

## Findings

The manuscript preserves the weapons concealment, Athena’s lamp, the handmaid’s
abuse, Penelope’s chair and fleece, Odysseus’s Aethon fiction, the twelve-day
Amnisus delay, the recognized clothing, the web’s three-year deception and fourth-
year betrayal, the bath, the boar scar, and the secrecy pact with Eurycleia.

The scar is not treated as a sudden sentimental reveal: Autolycus, the naming of
Odysseus, the Parnassus hunt, the wound, the basin’s fall, Athena’s distraction of
Penelope, and the nurse’s silence all remain causal steps. The final dream and
horn-and-ivory interpretation stay uncertain, while the twelve-axe test points
forward to the contest without importing its later violence.

## Beauty and cadence

The chapter moves from darkened metal and hidden weapons to the intimate sound of
the bronze basin, then widens into dream and prophecy. The long Cretan description
should feel persuasive enough to honor Penelope’s intelligence, while the scar scene
must contract into touch, ringing bronze, and command. The dream’s calm imagery
should make its threat more disturbing, not less.

## Open decisions

- Independently verify the twelve-day delay, clothing description, Autolycus episode,
  and all dream gates against the full Greek.
- Check the exact axe arrangement and the agency of Athena’s distraction.
- Commission an independent read-aloud and omissions/additions report.

**Conclusion:** recognition and contest architecture are preserved; not approved
for layout or final publication.
